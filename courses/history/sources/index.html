Primary and secondary sources

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

How to Study History · Lesson 2 of 4 · 6 min read

  • Primary sources come from the time period: letters, speeches, photos, laws, diaries.
  • Secondary sources are later interpretations: textbooks, historians' books, documentaries.

Sourcing a document (HIPP)

Historical context: what was happening? Intended audience: who was it for? Purpose: why was it made? Point of view: who made it, and how might that shape it?

A source can be biased and still useful. A politician's speech tells you what they wanted people to believe, which is valuable evidence in itself.
