How to Study History · Lesson 1 of 4 · 6 min read

History isn't a list of dates. It's a story of causes and effects, and of how people's lives changed.

Historical thinking skills

  • Causation: what led to an event, and what did it lead to? Separate long-term causes from immediate triggers.
  • Continuity and change: what changed over a period, and what stayed the same?
  • Comparison: how were two events, places, or groups similar and different?
  • Context: what was going on at the time that explains an event?
Tip For any event, try to answer the 5 W's plus "So what?": why does it matter for what came later?

Check your understanding

Which is an example of a long-term cause, rather than an immediate trigger?

  1. A single assassination
  2. Decades of rising tensions between alliances
  3. A specific battle
  4. A declaration of war
Show answer

Answer: B. Long-term causes build over years. Immediate triggers, like an assassination, set things off.
