Factoring and solving quadratics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Algebra 1 Essentials · Lesson 6 of 6 · 7 min read

A quadratic has an $x^2$ term: $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$. Its graph is a parabola, and its solutions are the x-intercepts.

Factoring $x^2 + bx + c$

Find two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$.

Example

$x^2 + 5x + 6$: the numbers 2 and 3 multiply to 6 and add to 5, so it factors to $(x + 2)(x + 3)$.

Zero product property

If $(x + 2)(x + 3) = 0$, then $x + 2 = 0$ or $x + 3 = 0$, so $x = -2$ or $x = -3$.

When factoring doesn't work

Quadratic formula: $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ tells you how many real solutions: positive means two, zero means one, negative means none.

Check your understanding

Solve $x^2 - x - 12 = 0$.

  1. $x = 4$ or $x = -3$
  2. $x = -4$ or $x = 3$
  3. $x = 6$ or $x = -2$
  4. $x = 12$ or $x = -1$
Show answer

Answer: A. $-4$ and $3$ multiply to $-12$ and add to $-1$, so $(x - 4)(x + 3) = 0$, giving $x = 4$ or $x = -3$. The graph crosses the x-axis at both.
