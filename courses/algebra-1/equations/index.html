Solving linear equations

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Algebra 1 Essentials · Lesson 2 of 6 · 6 min read

Whatever you do to one side of an equation, do to the other. Undo operations in reverse order: first addition and subtraction, then multiplication and division.
Example

Solve $4x - 7 = 2x + 9$.

  1. Get the variables on one side: subtract $2x$ from both sides, giving $2x - 7 = 9$.
  2. Add 7 to both sides: $2x = 16$.
  3. Divide by 2: $x = 8$.

Check: $4(8) - 7 = 25$ and $2(8) + 9 = 25$.

Special cases

  • If the variables cancel and you get something false, like $3 = 5$, there is no solution.
  • If they cancel and you get something always true, like $3 = 3$, there are infinitely many solutions.
Tip Always plug your answer back into the original equation. It takes ten seconds and catches most mistakes.

Check your understanding

Solve $5(x - 2) = 3x + 4$.

  1. $x = 3$
  2. $x = 7$
  3. $x = -3$
  4. $x = 14$
Show answer

Answer: B. Distribute: $5x - 10 = 3x + 4$. Subtract $3x$: $2x - 10 = 4$. Add 10: $2x = 14$, so $x = 7$.
