Expressions and order of operations

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Algebra 1 Essentials · Lesson 1 of 6 · 10 min read

An expression is a math phrase like $3x + 2$. You simplify expressions; you solve equations.

Order of operations

Parentheses, then exponents, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right.

Multiplication doesn't always come before division. In $12 \div 3 \times 2$, work left to right: $4 \times 2 = 8$.

Distributing and combining like terms

Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power: $4x$ and $-7x$ are like terms, but $4x$ and $4x^2$ are not.

Example

Simplify $3(x + 4) - 2x$.

Distribute: $3x + 12 - 2x$. Combine like terms: $x + 12$.

Tip A minus sign in front of parentheses flips every sign inside: $-(x - 5) = -x + 5$.

Check your understanding

Simplify $2(3x - 1) + 4x$.

  1. $10x - 2$
  2. $10x - 1$
  3. $6x + 3$
  4. $10x + 2$
Show answer

Answer: A. Distribute: $6x - 2 + 4x$. Combine like terms: $10x - 2$.
