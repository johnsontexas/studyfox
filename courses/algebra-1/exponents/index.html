Exponent rules

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Algebra 1 Essentials · Lesson 5 of 6 · 10 min read

RuleExample
$x^a \cdot x^b = x^{a+b}$$x^2 \cdot x^5 = x^7$
$\dfrac{x^a}{x^b} = x^{a-b}$$\dfrac{x^6}{x^2} = x^4$
$(x^a)^b = x^{ab}$$(x^3)^2 = x^6$
$x^0 = 1$$7^0 = 1$
$x^{-a} = \dfrac{1}{x^a}$$2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$
$x^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{x}$$27^{1/3} = 3$
Tip Multiply the same base: add exponents. Power of a power: multiply exponents. Mixing these two up is the most common exponent mistake.

Check your understanding

Simplify $(x^3)^2 \cdot x^4$.

  1. $x^9$
  2. $x^{10}$
  3. $x^{24}$
  4. $x^{12}$
Show answer

Answer: B. $(x^3)^2 = x^6$, and $x^6 \cdot x^4 = x^{10}$.
