Area and volume

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Data, Geometry, and Trig · Lesson 5 of 7 · 6 min read

The SAT gives you a reference sheet with every area and volume formula you need. Know where it is and use it.

Scaling: if every length is multiplied by $k$, area is multiplied by $k^2$ and volume by $k^3$.
Example

A cylinder has radius 3 and height 10. Its volume is $\pi r^2 h = \pi(9)(10) = 90\pi$.

If the radius doubles, the volume is multiplied by 4, giving $360\pi$.

Tip For composite shapes, split them into rectangles, triangles, and circles, find each area, and add or subtract.

Check your understanding

A cube has edges of length 3. If each edge is tripled, the new volume is how many times the original?

  1. 3
  2. 9
  3. 27
  4. 81
Show answer

Answer: C. Lengths multiplied by 3 means volume is multiplied by $3^3 = 27$.
