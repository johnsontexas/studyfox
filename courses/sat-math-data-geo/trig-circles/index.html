Right triangles, trig, and circles

Right triangles

Pythagorean theorem: $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Know the special right triangles (both are on the reference sheet):

  • $45$-$45$-$90$: sides $x$, $x$, $x\sqrt{2}$.
  • $30$-$60$-$90$: sides $x$, $x\sqrt{3}$, $2x$.

Trig ratios

SOH CAH TOA: $\sin = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\cos = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\tan = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$.

In a right triangle, $\sin(x^\circ) = \cos(90^\circ - x^\circ)$. This identity shows up on almost every test.

Circles

  • Equation: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, center $(h, k)$, radius $r$. If the equation is expanded, complete the square to find the center.
  • Arc length $= \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r$. Sector area $= \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot \pi r^2$.
  • Radians: $\pi$ radians $= 180^\circ$.

Check your understanding

In a right triangle, $\sin(x^\circ) = 0.6$. What is $\cos((90 - x)^\circ)$?

  1. 0.4
  2. 0.6
  3. 0.8
  4. 1.6
Show answer

Answer: B. Sine of an angle equals cosine of its complement, so $\cos((90 - x)^\circ) = 0.6$.
