Percentages

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Data, Geometry, and Trig · Lesson 1 of 7 · 7 min read

A percent change is $\frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100$. Always divide by the original value.
  • Increase by 20%: multiply by $1.2$. Decrease by 20%: multiply by $0.8$.
  • Back-to-back changes multiply: a 20% increase then a 20% decrease is $1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$, a 4% decrease overall.
  • "$p$ percent of $x$" is $\frac{p}{100}x$.
Example

After a 25% discount, a jacket costs \$60. What was the original price?

$0.75x = 60$, so $x = 80$. The original price was \$80.

Tip To find the original amount after a change, divide by the multiplier. Do not add the percent back on to the new price.

Check your understanding

A price rises from \$40 to \$50. What is the percent increase?

  1. 10%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 50%
Show answer

Answer: C. $\frac{50 - 40}{40} = \frac{10}{40} = 0.25$, or 25%.
