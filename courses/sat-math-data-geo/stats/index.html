Mean, median, and spread

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Data, Geometry, and Trig · Lesson 3 of 7 · 8 min read

  • Mean: the sum divided by the count. It is pulled toward outliers.
  • Median: the middle value when sorted. Outliers barely change it.
  • Range: maximum minus minimum.
  • Standard deviation: how spread out the data are around the mean. More bunched data means a smaller standard deviation.
Example

Data: 4, 5, 5, 6, 40. Mean $= 60 \div 5 = 12$. Median $= 5$. Removing 40 drops the mean to 5 but barely changes the median.

Tip If the question gives a mean and asks for a missing value, use the total: mean times count equals sum.

Samples and surveys

A result can only be generalized to the population the sample was randomly drawn from. A larger random sample gives a smaller margin of error. A survey of one school's students tells you about that school, not the whole country.

Check your understanding

Five test scores have a mean of 82. Four of them are 80, 85, 78, and 90. What is the fifth?

  1. 75
  2. 77
  3. 82
  4. 85
Show answer

Answer: B. Total $= 82 \times 5 = 410$. The four known scores sum to 333, so the fifth is $410 - 333 = 77$.
