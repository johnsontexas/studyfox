Unit 1: Renaissance and Exploration

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP European History · Lesson 2 of 10 · 8 min read

Covers about 1450 to 1648.

The Italian Renaissance

  • Humanism: studying classical Greek and Roman texts and focusing on human potential (Petrarch). Civic humanism trained citizens for public life.
  • Wealthy city-states and patrons (the Medici in Florence) funded art. Artists used perspective and realism (Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael).
  • Machiavelli's The Prince described politics as it is, not as it should be.

The Northern Renaissance

Christian humanists (Erasmus, Thomas More) applied humanist study to religion. The printing press (Gutenberg, about 1450) spread ideas quickly and raised literacy.

New monarchies

Rulers in France, England, and Spain centralized power: standing armies, taxes, and less reliance on nobles.

Exploration

Portugal and Spain led, motivated by "gold, God, and glory." Results: the Columbian Exchange, the Commercial Revolution (joint-stock companies, banking), the Price Revolution (inflation from American silver), and the Atlantic slave trade.
