Unit 5: Conflict, Crisis, and Reaction (late 1700s)

AP European History · Lesson 6 of 10 · 8 min read

The French Revolution

  1. Causes: debt (partly from helping the American Revolution), an unfair tax system (the Third Estate paid most taxes), Enlightenment ideas, and bread shortages.
  2. Liberal phase (1789 to 1792): the Estates-General became the National Assembly, the Tennis Court Oath, the storming of the Bastille, the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, and a constitutional monarchy.
  3. Radical phase (1792 to 1794): France became a republic, Louis XVI was executed, and Robespierre's Reign of Terror executed thousands.
  4. The Directory (1795 to 1799), then Napoleon.

Napoleon

He centralized France, signed the Concordat with the Church, and created the Napoleonic Code (legal equality for men, but limited rights for women). His conquests spread revolutionary ideas and sparked nationalism in the lands he conquered. Defeated in Russia (1812) and at Waterloo (1815).

Reaction

The Congress of Vienna (1815), led by Metternich, restored monarchies and a balance of power. Romanticism reacted against Enlightenment reason with emotion and nature.
