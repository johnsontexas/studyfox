Unit 4: Science, Enlightenment, and Politics

AP European History · Lesson 5 of 10 · 8 min read

The Scientific Revolution

Copernicus (a sun-centered universe), Kepler (elliptical orbits), Galileo (telescope observations; tried by the Church), and Newton (laws of motion and gravity). Bacon promoted the empirical, inductive method; Descartes promoted deductive reasoning ("I think, therefore I am").

The Enlightenment

  • Locke: natural rights (life, liberty, property); government by consent.
  • Hobbes: people need a strong ruler to escape a brutish state of nature.
  • Montesquieu: separation of powers. Voltaire: religious tolerance, free speech. Rousseau: the social contract and the general will.
  • Adam Smith: laissez-faire economics (The Wealth of Nations, 1776).
  • Mary Wollstonecraft: education and rights for women.
  • Ideas spread through salons, coffeehouses, and the Encyclopédie. Deism pictured God as a clockmaker.

Enlightened absolutism

Some monarchs adopted reforms while keeping full power: Frederick the Great (Prussia), Catherine the Great (Russia), and Joseph II (Austria).
