Unit 2: The Age of Reformation

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP European History · Lesson 3 of 10 · 8 min read

  • Martin Luther (95 Theses, 1517) attacked the sale of indulgences and taught salvation by faith alone and scripture as the sole authority.
  • John Calvin taught predestination; Calvinism spread to Switzerland, France (Huguenots), the Netherlands, and Scotland.
  • England: Henry VIII broke with Rome to annul his marriage, creating the Church of England (1534). Elizabeth I settled on a moderate Protestant church.
  • Anabaptists rejected infant baptism and were persecuted by both Catholics and Protestants.

The Catholic (Counter-) Reformation

The Council of Trent (1545 to 1563) reaffirmed Catholic doctrine and fixed abuses. The Jesuits spread Catholicism through education and missions. The Index of Prohibited Books and the Inquisition fought heresy.

Wars of religion

  • The French Wars of Religion ended with the Edict of Nantes (1598), which tolerated Huguenots.
  • The Peace of Augsburg (1555) let German princes choose Lutheranism or Catholicism.
  • The Thirty Years' War (1618 to 1648) devastated Germany. The Peace of Westphalia recognized Calvinism and established the principle of state sovereignty.

  3. Created the Church of England
