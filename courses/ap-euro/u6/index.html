Unit 6: Industrialization and Its Effects

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP European History · Lesson 7 of 10 · 8 min read

Covers about 1815 to 1914.

  • Britain industrialized first: coal and iron, capital, colonies and markets, and workers freed by the Agricultural Revolution. Textiles, the steam engine, and railroads led.
  • Continental Europe followed, often with more government help. The Second Industrial Revolution (after 1870) brought steel, chemicals, electricity, and the internal combustion engine.

Social effects

  • Rapid urbanization caused crowding, disease, and pollution, leading to reforms like sewers and public health laws.
  • A new middle class and an industrial working class. The "separate spheres" ideal confined middle-class women to the home.
  • Factory and child labor laws (Britain's Factory Act of 1833).

New ideologies

  • Liberalism: individual rights, constitutions, free markets.
  • Conservatism: tradition, monarchy, and established churches (Burke, Metternich).
  • Socialism: utopian socialists (Owen, Fourier), then Marx: history is class struggle, and workers (the proletariat) would overthrow capitalists.
  • Nationalism: loyalty to a people sharing a language and culture.
