Conflicting viewpoints

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT Science · Lesson 4 of 4 · 7 min read

This passage gives two or more explanations of the same thing. It is the most reading-heavy science passage, so many students save it for last.

  1. Read the introduction to learn the question being debated.
  2. For each viewpoint, write a one-line summary of its main claim.
  3. Note where the viewpoints agree as well as where they differ. Questions often ask what both would accept.
Example

Scientist 1 says a crater was made by a meteor. Scientist 2 says it was made by a volcano. Both agree the crater is about 50,000 years old.

A question asking "Which would both scientists agree with?" points to the age, not the cause.

Tip When a question asks which finding would weaken a viewpoint, look for evidence that contradicts its main claim.

Check your understanding

Scientist 1 says birds descended from dinosaurs. Scientist 2 says birds and dinosaurs share an older common ancestor. Which finding would most weaken Scientist 1's view?

  1. A bird-like fossil older than any known dinosaur
  2. A dinosaur with feathers
  3. A modern bird with strong legs
  4. A dinosaur that laid eggs
Show answer

Answer: A. If a bird-like animal existed before any dinosaur, birds could not have descended from dinosaurs, which undercuts Scientist 1.
