Equivalent expressions

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Algebra and Advanced Math · Lesson 6 of 6 · 7 min read

These questions ask which expression is equivalent to the one given. Know the main rules:

RuleExample
Multiply powers: add exponents$x^3 \cdot x^4 = x^7$
Power of a power: multiply exponents$(x^3)^4 = x^{12}$
Divide powers: subtract exponents$\frac{x^7}{x^2} = x^5$
Fractional exponents are roots$x^{1/2} = \sqrt{x}$, $x^{2/3} = \sqrt[3]{x^2}$
Difference of squares$a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$
Perfect square$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
Tip The fastest check: pick a value like $x = 2$, evaluate the original and each choice, and keep the one that matches. Avoid 0 and 1, since too many expressions agree there.
Incorrect$(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 9$. This forgets the middle term $6x$.
Correct$(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$.

Check your understanding

Which is equivalent to $\frac{(x^3)^2}{x^4}$?

  1. $x$
  2. $x^2$
  3. $x^5$
  4. $x^{10}$
Show answer

Answer: B. $(x^3)^2 = x^6$, and $\frac{x^6}{x^4} = x^2$.
