Systems of linear equations

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Algebra and Advanced Math · Lesson 2 of 6 · 8 min read

A system's solution is the point where the lines meet. There are three ways to solve:

  • Substitution: best when one equation is already solved for a variable.
  • Elimination: add or subtract the equations to cancel a variable.
  • Desmos: graph both and click the intersection.

How many solutions?

LinesSolutionsSlopes and intercepts
Cross onceOneDifferent slopes
ParallelNoneSame slope, different intercepts
Same lineInfinitely manySame slope, same intercept
Example

$2x + 3y = 12$ and $4x + ky = 20$ have no solution. Find $k$.

Multiply the first by 2: $4x + 6y = 24$. For parallel lines the $y$ coefficients must match, so $k = 6$. The constants (24 and 20) differ, so the lines never meet.

Tip If a question asks for $x + y$ or another combination, try adding or subtracting the equations directly before solving for each variable.

Check your understanding

If $x + y = 10$ and $x - y = 4$, what is $x$?

  1. 3
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 14
Show answer

Answer: C. Add the equations: $2x = 14$, so $x = 7$.
