Quadratics in every form

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Math: Algebra and Advanced Math · Lesson 4 of 6 · 9 min read

The SAT uses three forms of a quadratic. Each one shows a different feature of the parabola.

FormExampleShows you
Standard$y = x^2 - 6x + 5$The y-intercept (5)
Factored$y = (x - 1)(x - 5)$The x-intercepts (1 and 5)
Vertex$y = (x - 3)^2 - 4$The vertex $(3, -4)$

Useful facts

  • The vertex's x-coordinate is $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$, halfway between the x-intercepts.
  • The sum of the solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ is $-\frac{b}{a}$, and their product is $\frac{c}{a}$.
  • The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ tells how many real solutions there are: positive means two, zero means one, negative means none.
Example

For what value of $c$ does $x^2 + 8x + c = 0$ have exactly one real solution?

Set the discriminant to zero: $64 - 4c = 0$, so $c = 16$.

Tip When the equation is messy, graph it in Desmos and click the x-intercepts.

Check your understanding

What is the vertex of $y = (x + 2)^2 - 7$?

  1. $(2, -7)$
  2. $(-2, -7)$
  3. $(2, 7)$
  4. $(-2, 7)$
Show answer

Answer: B. Vertex form $(x - h)^2 + k$ has vertex $(h, k)$. Here $x + 2 = x - (-2)$, so the vertex is $(-2, -7)$.
