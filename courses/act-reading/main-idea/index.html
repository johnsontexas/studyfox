Main idea and author's purpose

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT Reading · Lesson 3 of 4 · 6 min read

Main idea questions ask about the whole passage or a whole paragraph. The right answer is broad enough to cover everything, but not so broad that it could describe a different passage.

Too narrowJust rightToo broad
"The narrator's mother grew roses.""The narrator reflects on how her mother's garden shaped her childhood.""Gardening is important to many families."

Purpose and function

"The main purpose of the third paragraph is to..." asks why the paragraph is there. Common jobs: introduce a problem, give an example, present a counterargument, or shift to a new time period.

Tip Use your paragraph summaries from the first read. They are often enough to answer main idea questions without rereading.

Check your understanding

A passage describes several scientists' failed attempts to measure a star and then one scientist's success. Which is the best main idea?

  1. Measuring stars is impossible.
  2. After many failures, one scientist found a way to measure the star.
  3. All scientists use telescopes.
  4. The star is very far away.
Show answer

Answer: B. This choice covers both the failed attempts and the success, which make up the whole passage.
