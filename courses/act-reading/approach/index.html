Reading a long passage fast

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT Reading · Lesson 1 of 4 · 7 min read

ACT Reading uses long passages, each with several questions, from four areas: literary narrative, social science, humanities, and natural science. On the enhanced ACT you have 40 minutes for 36 questions.

A plan that works

  1. Read actively, but quickly. After each paragraph, think of a few words that sum it up ("mom's childhood," "first study," "critics disagree").
  2. Don't try to memorize details. You can always go back. The questions often give line or paragraph numbers.
  3. Answer from the passage, not memory. For any detail question, find the sentence that proves your answer.
Tip Start with the passage type you are best at. You don't have to do the passages in order.
Every correct answer is directly supported by the passage. If you have to add your own assumptions, the choice is wrong.

Check your understanding

What should you do after reading each paragraph?

  1. Memorize every name and date
  2. Note a short summary of its main point
  3. Skip to the questions immediately
  4. Reread it three times
Show answer

Answer: B. A quick summary of each paragraph builds a map of the passage, so you know where to look when questions ask about details.
