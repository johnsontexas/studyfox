Transitions and organization

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT English · Lesson 5 of 6 · 6 min read

Transition questions work the same way on the ACT as on the SAT: identify the relationship between the ideas, then choose the word that signals it (contrast, result, addition, example, time).

Sentence placement

When asked where a sentence belongs, look for links: a pronoun that needs an earlier noun ("this method," "she"), a time word ("later," "first"), or an idea that continues the one before.

Opening and closing sentences

A good paragraph opener introduces what the paragraph is about. A good closer wraps up the paragraph or leads into the next one. Read the whole paragraph before you choose.

Tip Watch out for a transition that is technically logical but connects the wrong two ideas.

Check your understanding

A sentence begins, "This discovery changed how scientists studied the reef." Where should it most likely go?

  1. As the first sentence of the essay
  2. Right after a sentence that describes a discovery
  3. Right before the sentence that describes the discovery
  4. At the end of an unrelated paragraph
Show answer

Answer: B. "This discovery" points back to something already described, so the sentence must come right after the discovery is mentioned.
