Commas, semicolons, colons, and dashes

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT English · Lesson 2 of 6 · 9 min read

MarkUse it toNeeds
Period / semicolonJoin two complete sentencesIndependent clause on both sides
Comma + FANBOYSJoin two complete sentencesFor, and, nor, but, or, yet, so
ColonIntroduce a list, explanation, or quoteIndependent clause before it
Pair of commas or dashesSet off extra informationThe same mark on both ends
IncorrectThe storm passed, the streets were flooded. (comma splice)
CorrectThe storm passed; the streets were flooded.
CorrectThe storm passed, but the streets were flooded.

Extra information

If you can remove a phrase and the sentence still makes sense and keeps its meaning, the phrase is nonessential and needs commas (or dashes) on both sides.

IncorrectMy sister, a nurse at Mercy Hospital works nights.
CorrectMy sister, a nurse at Mercy Hospital, works nights.
Never put a single comma between a subject and its verb, or between a verb and its object.

Check your understanding

Which is punctuated correctly?

  1. I love the ocean, I go every summer.
  2. I love the ocean; I go every summer.
  3. I love the ocean: and I go every summer.
  4. I love the ocean I go every summer.
Show answer

Answer: B. Both parts are complete sentences, so a semicolon joins them. The first is a comma splice and the last is a run-on.
