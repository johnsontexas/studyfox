"Should the writer add this?"

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT English · Lesson 6 of 6 · 6 min read

These questions describe a possible addition or deletion and ask whether the writer should make it, with a reason attached to each choice.

  1. Decide yes or no first. Does the sentence fit the paragraph's focus? If it drifts to another topic, the answer is no.
  2. Then check the reason. Two choices say yes and two say no. Pick the one whose reason accurately describes the sentence and the paragraph.
A sentence can be interesting and true and still be wrong to add if it doesn't support the paragraph's main point.

"Did the essay accomplish its goal?"

At the end of a passage, you may be asked whether the essay met a specific goal. Compare the goal to what the essay actually focused on. If the goal is "describe the history of jazz" and the essay is about one musician's childhood, the answer is no.

Check your understanding

A paragraph explains how bees make honey. The writer considers adding a sentence about a famous beekeeper's birthday. Should the writer add it?

  1. Yes, because it adds an interesting detail.
  2. Yes, because it explains how honey is made.
  3. No, because it doesn't relate to how bees make honey.
  4. No, because beekeepers are never mentioned.
Show answer

Answer: C. The paragraph's focus is how honey is made, and a birthday doesn't support that point.
