Solve any equation by graphing

Desmos for the SAT · Lesson 2 of 7 · 5 min read

For an equation in one variable, you don't have to do the algebra. Two ways work:

  1. Type the equation exactly as written, like 3(x-2)=2x+7. Desmos draws a vertical line at each solution.
  2. Graph each side separately: y=3(x-2) and y=2x+7. The solution is the x-coordinate of the intersection.
The solution to an equation is the x-value where the two sides are equal, so read the x-coordinate of the intersection, not the y-coordinate.

This works just as well for equations that are painful by hand, like $\sqrt{x+7} = x - 5$ or $|2x - 3| = 7$.

Tip If the answer choices are numbers, you can also type each choice into the equation to check it. Desmos shows "true" or "false".

Check your understanding

You graph $y = 3(x - 2)$ and $y = 2x + 7$. They intersect at $(13, 33)$. What is the solution to $3(x - 2) = 2x + 7$?

  1. $x = 33$
  2. $x = 13$
  3. $x = 7$
  4. $x = 46$
Show answer

Answer: B. The solution is the x-coordinate of the intersection, $x = 13$. Check: $3(11) = 33$ and $2(13) + 7 = 33$.
