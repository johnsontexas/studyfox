Getting around Desmos

Desmos for the SAT · Lesson 1 of 7 · 5 min read

Desmos is built into every math module of the digital SAT, so you can use it on every math question. Learning a handful of moves now saves minutes on test day.

Typing math

  • Fractions: type / and Desmos builds a fraction. Use the right arrow key to leave the denominator.
  • Exponents: type ^, for example x^2. Press the right arrow to get back to the baseline.
  • Square roots and pi: type sqrt and pi, and Desmos turns them into symbols.
  • Multiplication: write 3x or 3*x. Use parentheses generously: 3(x-2).

Reading the graph

  • Click a curve and gray dots appear at its x-intercepts, y-intercept, vertex, and intersections. Click a dot to see its exact coordinates.
  • Use the zoom buttons or pinch to zoom. The house icon resets the view.
  • If a graph seems to be missing, it's usually off screen. Zoom out.

Check your understanding

You graph $y = x^2 - 6x + 5$ and click the curve. Which point is the vertex?

  1. $(1, 0)$
  2. $(3, -4)$
  3. $(5, 0)$
  4. $(0, 5)$
Show answer

Answer: B. The vertex is the lowest point of this upward parabola, at $x = 3$: $9 - 18 + 5 = -4$. The points $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$ are the x-intercepts, and $(0, 5)$ is the y-intercept.
