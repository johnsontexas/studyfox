Counting and probability

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

ACT Math · Lesson 6 of 6 · 7 min read

Fundamental counting principle: if one choice has $m$ options and another has $n$, together there are $m \times n$ combinations.
  • Permutations (order matters): $_nP_r = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}$. Example: choosing a president and vice president from 10 people: $10 \times 9 = 90$.
  • Combinations (order doesn't matter): $_nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n - r)!}$. Example: choosing a 2-person committee from 10 people: $\frac{90}{2} = 45$.

Probability

  • "And" for independent events: multiply. The probability of two heads is $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$.
  • "Not": subtract from 1. The probability of at least one head in 3 flips is $1 - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{7}{8}$.
  • Without replacement, the second probability changes because one item is gone.

A restaurant offers 4 appetizers, 6 entrées, and 3 desserts. How many different three-course meals are possible?

  1. 13
  2. 24
  3. 72
