Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 1 · Lesson 3 of 8 · 8 min read

  • Work: $W = Fd\cos\theta$. The work-energy theorem: net work equals the change in kinetic energy.
  • Kinetic energy: $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Gravitational potential energy: $U_g = mgh$. Spring potential energy: $U_s = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.
  • Conservation of energy: in a closed system with no friction, total mechanical energy is constant. Friction turns mechanical energy into internal (thermal) energy.
  • Power: $P = \frac{W}{t} = Fv$.
Example

A 0.5 kg ball drops from 5 m. Speed at the bottom: $mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{98} \approx 9.9$ m/s.

Check your understanding

A ball's speed doubles. Its kinetic energy:

  1. Doubles
  2. Quadruples
  3. Stays the same
  4. Halves
Show answer

Answer: B. Kinetic energy depends on $v^2$, so doubling v multiplies K by 4.
