Unit 4: Linear Momentum

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 1 · Lesson 4 of 8 · 7 min read

  • Momentum: $p = mv$, a vector.
  • Impulse: $J = F\Delta t = \Delta p$. The area under a force vs. time graph is impulse. Airbags increase $\Delta t$ to reduce force.
  • Conservation of momentum: if no net external force acts, total momentum is constant in every collision and explosion.
  • Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy too. Inelastic collisions lose kinetic energy; perfectly inelastic collisions have the objects stick together.
  • Center of mass of an isolated system moves at constant velocity.
Example

A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s hits a stopped 1500 kg car, and they stick. $1000(20) = 2500v$, so $v = 8$ m/s.

Check your understanding

In which type of collision is kinetic energy conserved?

  1. Perfectly inelastic
  2. Inelastic
  3. Elastic
  4. All collisions
Show answer

Answer: C. Momentum is conserved in all collisions, but kinetic energy is conserved only in elastic ones.
