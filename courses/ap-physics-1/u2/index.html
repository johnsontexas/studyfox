Unit 2: Force and Translational Dynamics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 1 · Lesson 2 of 8 · 9 min read

  • Newton's laws: (1) objects keep their velocity unless a net force acts; (2) $\Sigma F = ma$; (3) forces come in equal and opposite pairs on different objects.
  • Free-body diagrams: draw every force on one object: gravity ($mg$), normal force, tension, friction, applied forces.
  • Friction: $f_k = \mu_k N$; static friction $f_s \le \mu_s N$.
  • Inclines: the gravity component along the incline is $mg\sin\theta$; perpendicular is $mg\cos\theta$.
  • Circular motion: centripetal acceleration $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$ points toward the center. Some real force (tension, gravity, friction) provides it.
  • Gravitation: $F = \frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}$.
  • Springs: $F = -kx$ (Hooke's law).
Centripetal force is not a new force. Never draw it on a free-body diagram; it is the net of the real forces pointing toward the center.

A 2 kg box is pushed with a net force of 10 N. What is its acceleration?

  1. 0.2 m/s²
  2. 5 m/s²
  3. 12 m/s²

Answer: B. $a = F/m = 10/2 = 5$ m/s².
