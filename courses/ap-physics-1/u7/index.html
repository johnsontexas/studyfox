Unit 7: Oscillations

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 1 · Lesson 7 of 8 · 7 min read

  • Simple harmonic motion occurs when a restoring force is proportional to displacement.
  • Mass on a spring: $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}$. Pendulum (small angles): $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$. A pendulum's period does not depend on mass or amplitude.
  • Energy: at the extremes, all energy is potential and speed is zero. At equilibrium, all energy is kinetic and speed is maximum.
  • Frequency $f = \frac{1}{T}$.

Check your understanding

To double a pendulum's period, you should:

  1. Double its length
  2. Quadruple its length
  3. Double its mass
  4. Double its amplitude
Show answer

Answer: B. T is proportional to $\sqrt{L}$, so T doubles when L is multiplied by 4.
