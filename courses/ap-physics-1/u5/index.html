Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 1 · Lesson 5 of 8 · 8 min read

  • Rotational kinematics: θ, ω, α replace x, v, a. $v = r\omega$, $a_t = r\alpha$.
  • Torque: $\tau = rF\sin\theta$. Pushing farther from the axis or perpendicular to the lever arm gives more torque.
  • Rotational inertia (I) depends on mass and how far it is from the axis. More mass far from the axis means larger I.
  • Newton's second law for rotation: $\Sigma\tau = I\alpha$.
  • Equilibrium: both net force and net torque are zero. Choose a pivot that removes unknown forces from the torque equation.

Why is it easier to open a door by pushing at the handle than near the hinges?

  1. The handle has less friction
  2. A larger lever arm gives more torque for the same force
  3. The door weighs less at the handle
  4. Rotational inertia is lower at the handle
Show answer

Answer: B. Torque = rF, so pushing farther from the hinge (larger r) creates more torque with the same force.
