Africa, Asia, Indigenous Americas, and the Pacific

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Art History · Lesson 5 of 5 · 9 min read

  • Indigenous Americas: Chavín, the Templo Mayor (Aztec), Machu Picchu (Inca), Mesa Verde cliff dwellings, and Plains ledger drawings.
  • Africa: the Great Mosque of Djenné (mud brick, rebuilt by the community), Benin bronze plaques, Kuba and Yoruba masks used in performances, and power figures (nkisi n'kondi). Many works are meant to be experienced in use, not in museums.
  • West and Central Asia: the Dome of the Rock, the Great Mosque of Isfahan, and Islamic art's calligraphy and geometric patterns.
  • South, East, and Southeast Asia: the Great Stupa at Sanchi, Angkor Wat, the Terracotta Army, Japanese woodblock prints (Hokusai's Great Wave), and Chinese landscape painting.
  • The Pacific: Moai on Rapa Nui, Maori meeting houses, and navigation charts from the Marshall Islands.
  • Global contemporary (1980 to now): art about identity, globalization, and technology (Maya Lin's Vietnam Veterans Memorial, Kara Walker, El Anatsui).

Why is the Great Mosque of Djenné re-plastered every year?

  1. It is made of marble that stains
  2. Its mud brick needs annual renewal, done as a community festival
  3. It is rebuilt in a new location each year
  4. It is a temporary structure
Show answer

Answer: B. Rain erodes the mud plaster, so the community renews it each year, making upkeep part of the building's meaning.
