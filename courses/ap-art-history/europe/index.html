Early Europe and Colonial Americas

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Art History · Lesson 3 of 5 · 8 min read

  • Early Christian and Byzantine: catacomb paintings, mosaics in San Vitale (Justinian and Theodora joining political and religious power), and Hagia Sophia.
  • Romanesque and Gothic: pilgrimage churches with round arches; Gothic cathedrals (Chartres) with pointed arches, rib vaults, flying buttresses, and stained glass that filled interiors with light.
  • Renaissance: linear perspective, classical revival, and humanism (Masaccio, Leonardo's Last Supper, Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel). Northern artists used oil paint for fine detail (the Arnolfini Portrait).
  • Baroque: drama, motion, and strong light and dark contrast (Caravaggio, Bernini) to serve the Counter-Reformation. Dutch art showed daily life for a merchant market (Vermeer, Rembrandt).
  • Colonial Americas: works blending European and Indigenous traditions, like casta paintings and the Virgin of Guadalupe images.
