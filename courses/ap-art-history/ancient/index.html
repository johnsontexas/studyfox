Global prehistory and the ancient Mediterranean

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Art History · Lesson 2 of 5 · 8 min read

  • Global prehistory (about 30,000 to 500 BCE): cave paintings (Lascaux), the Venus of Willendorf, Stonehenge. Art tied to survival, ritual, and the earliest settled societies.
  • Ancient Near East: the White Temple and ziggurat of Uruk, the Standard of Ur, the Code of Hammurabi stele (law with divine authority), Assyrian lamassu.
  • Egypt: art for the afterlife and eternal kingship. The pyramids of Giza, the Palette of King Narmer (hierarchical scale), and the Amarna period's brief shift to more naturalistic forms under Akhenaten.
  • Greece: from stiff Archaic kouros figures to Classical balance and contrapposto (the Doryphoros), then Hellenistic drama and emotion (the Nike of Samothrace).
  • Etruscan and Roman: realistic portraits (veristic busts), the Pantheon's concrete dome, and art serving imperial propaganda (Augustus of Prima Porta).
