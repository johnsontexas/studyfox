Unit 6: Market Failure and the Role of Government

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Microeconomics · Lesson 6 of 6 · 8 min read

  • Externalities: costs or benefits to third parties. Negative (pollution): the market overproduces, so a per-unit (Pigouvian) tax fixes it. Positive (vaccines, education): the market underproduces, so a subsidy fixes it.
  • Socially optimal output is where marginal social benefit = marginal social cost.
  • Public goods are nonrival and nonexcludable (national defense). The free-rider problem means markets underprovide them.
  • Inequality: the Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient measure it. Progressive taxes (higher rates on higher incomes) and transfers reduce it.
  • Government failure: policies can also create inefficiency.

Check your understanding

A factory's pollution harms nearby residents. Without government action, the market will:

  1. Produce the socially optimal amount
  2. Overproduce the good
  3. Underproduce the good
  4. Stop producing the good
Show answer

Answer: B. The firm ignores the external cost, so marginal private cost is below marginal social cost and output is too high.
