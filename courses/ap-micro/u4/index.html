Unit 4: Imperfect Competition

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Microeconomics · Lesson 4 of 6 · 9 min read

  • Monopoly: one seller, barriers to entry. MR lies below demand. It produces where MR = MC and charges the price on the demand curve above that point. Result: higher price, lower output, deadweight loss.
  • Natural monopoly: economies of scale make one firm cheapest (utilities). Regulators may set price at ATC (fair return) or MC (socially optimal, but may need a subsidy).
  • Price discrimination: charging different customers different prices. Perfect price discrimination removes consumer surplus and deadweight loss.
  • Monopolistic competition: many firms with differentiated products (restaurants). Zero economic profit in the long run, but excess capacity.
  • Oligopoly: a few interdependent firms. Analyzed with game theory: find each player's dominant strategy and the Nash equilibrium.
Tip For a payoff matrix, check one player at a time: "If the other picks A, what is my best choice? If the other picks B?" If the answer is the same both times, that's a dominant strategy.

Compared with perfect competition, a monopoly produces:

  1. More output at a lower price
  2. Less output at a higher price
  3. The same output at the same price
  4. More output at a higher price
Show answer

Answer: B. Because MR is below demand, the monopolist restricts output and charges a higher price, creating deadweight loss.
