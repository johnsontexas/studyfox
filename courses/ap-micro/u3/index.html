Unit 3: Production, Cost, and Perfect Competition

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Microeconomics · Lesson 3 of 6 · 10 min read

  • Diminishing marginal returns: adding workers to fixed capital eventually adds less output each time.
  • Costs: fixed (don't change with output), variable, total, and average (ATC, AVC, AFC). MC crosses ATC and AVC at their minimums.
  • Profit maximization: every firm produces where MR = MC.
  • Perfect competition: many firms, identical products, price takers, so P = MR = demand for each firm.
  • Shut-down rule: in the short run, shut down if price falls below minimum AVC.
  • Long run: economic profits attract entry and losses cause exit, until P = minimum ATC and economic profit is zero.
  • Perfect competition is allocatively efficient (P = MC) and productively efficient (P = min ATC) in the long run.
Economic profit subtracts both explicit and implicit (opportunity) costs. Zero economic profit means a normal profit, so firms are happy to stay.

Check your understanding

A perfectly competitive firm's price is below its average variable cost. In the short run it should:

  1. Keep producing to cover fixed costs
  2. Shut down
  3. Raise its price
  4. Produce more
Show answer

Answer: B. If price doesn't cover AVC, each unit loses money beyond fixed costs, so shutting down minimizes losses.
