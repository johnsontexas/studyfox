Unit 4: Parties, Elections, and Citizen Organizations

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Comparative Government · Lesson 5 of 6 · 7 min read

  • Electoral systems: single-member district plurality (first past the post: UK Commons, Nigeria's legislature) tends to create two-party systems. Proportional representation (Russia's Duma uses a mix; Mexico's legislature is mixed) allows more parties.
  • Party systems: one-party (China), dominant-party (Russia's United Russia; Mexico's PRI before 2000), two-party (UK historically), and multiparty.
  • Interest groups: pluralism (groups compete freely, as in the UK) vs. corporatism (the state recognizes and works with official groups, as in China and historically Mexico).
  • Social movements: environmental movements, Mexico's Zapatistas, Nigeria's #EndSARS protests against police abuse.
  • Elections in authoritarian states: held to show legitimacy, but limited by vetting candidates (Iran's Guardian Council), controlling media, and harassing opponents (Russia).
