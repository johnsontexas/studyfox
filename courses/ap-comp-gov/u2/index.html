Unit 2: Political Institutions

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Comparative Government · Lesson 3 of 6 · 8 min read

  • Parliamentary systems (UK): the executive comes from the legislature; a vote of no confidence can remove the government. Fusion of powers.
  • Presidential systems (Mexico, Nigeria): separately elected president and legislature, fixed terms. Separation of powers.
  • Semi-presidential (Russia): both a president and a prime minister. In Russia the president dominates.
  • Head of state vs. head of government: in the UK the monarch is head of state and the PM is head of government. In Iran the Supreme Leader outranks the president.
  • Legislatures: the UK House of Commons is powerful; the House of Lords can only delay. China's National People's Congress mostly approves party decisions.
  • Judiciaries: independence varies. The UK Supreme Court (2009) cannot strike down acts of Parliament (parliamentary sovereignty). Iran's Guardian Council vets laws and candidates.
  • Term limits: Mexico's president serves one 6-year term. Russia's 2020 constitutional changes allowed Putin to stay until 2036.

In a parliamentary system, how can the government be removed between elections?

  1. Impeachment by the Supreme Court
  2. A vote of no confidence in the legislature
  3. A referendum only

Answer: B. Because the executive depends on the legislature's support, losing a confidence vote forces it to resign or call an election.
