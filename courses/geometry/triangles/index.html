Triangles and the Pythagorean theorem

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Geometry Essentials · Lesson 2 of 5 · 6 min read

The angles of any triangle add to $180^\circ$. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior angles farthest from it.

Pythagorean theorem

In a right triangle with legs $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$ (the side across from the right angle): $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.

Common triples worth memorizing: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15).

Special right triangles

  • 45-45-90: sides $x$, $x$, $x\sqrt{2}$.
  • 30-60-90: sides $x$, $x\sqrt{3}$, $2x$. The shortest side is across from $30^\circ$.

Check your understanding

A right triangle has legs of 6 and 8. How long is the hypotenuse?

  1. $10$
  2. $14$
  3. $\sqrt{28}$
  4. $48$
Show answer

Answer: A. $6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$, and $\sqrt{100} = 10$. It's a 3-4-5 triangle doubled.
