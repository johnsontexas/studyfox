Electric potential

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism · Lesson 2 of 6 · 8 min read

  • $\Delta V = -\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{r}$ and $E_x = -\frac{dV}{dx}$.
  • For a point charge: $V = \frac{kq}{r}$ (zero at infinity). Potential is a scalar, so add contributions without vectors.
  • Continuous distributions: $V = \int \frac{k\,dq}{r}$.
  • Work by the field: $W = -q\Delta V$; potential energy of two charges $U = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r}$.
Example

If $V(x) = 3x^2$ volts, then $E_x = -\frac{dV}{dx} = -6x$ V/m.

Check your understanding

If $V(x) = 5x^3$, what is $E_x$?

  1. $15x^2$
  2. $-15x^2$
  3. $-5x^3$
  4. $\frac{5}{4}x^4$
Show answer

Answer: B. $E_x = -\frac{dV}{dx} = -15x^2$.
