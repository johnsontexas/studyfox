Electromagnetic induction and inductors

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism · Lesson 6 of 6 · 9 min read

  • Faraday's law: $\varepsilon = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$, with $\Phi_B = \int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A}$. Lenz's law gives the direction.
  • Inductance: $\varepsilon = -L\frac{dI}{dt}$. An inductor resists changes in current.
  • RL circuits: current grows as $I(t) = \frac{\varepsilon}{R}(1 - e^{-Rt/L})$; time constant $\tau = \frac{L}{R}$.
  • Energy in an inductor: $U = \frac{1}{2}LI^2$.
  • LC circuits oscillate with $\omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$.
  • Maxwell's equations summarize all of E&M.
Inductors are the opposite of capacitors in the moment after a switch closes: an inductor acts like an open circuit at first and like a wire at steady state.

What is the time constant of an RL circuit?
