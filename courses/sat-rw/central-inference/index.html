Central ideas and inferences

SAT Reading and Writing · Lesson 3 of 6 · 8 min read

Central idea questions

The main idea covers the whole passage, not one sentence of it. Many passages follow a pattern: an old belief, then new evidence that changes it. In that case, the main idea is the new view.

IncorrectA choice that is true but only describes one detail.
IncorrectA choice that goes further than the text ("always," "only," "proves").
CorrectA choice that sums up what the passage as a whole is saying.

Inference questions

These end with an unfinished sentence: "Which choice most logically completes the text?" The answer is the conclusion the evidence leads to and nothing more.

  1. Find what changed and what stayed the same. If two groups were treated the same except for one thing, that one thing explains the difference.
  2. Predict the ending in your own words before reading the choices.
  3. Reject choices that bring in outside ideas or make a stronger claim than the evidence supports.
Example

Two rows of beans got the same water, but one was in the sun and one in shade. The sunny row grew twice as tall. This suggests that...

Only sunlight differed, so the answer is that sunlight affected growth. A choice like "shade kills bean plants" goes too far.

Check your understanding

A passage says scientists believed X, but new studies found Y. What is the main idea most likely to be?

  1. Scientists believed X.
  2. New evidence suggests Y, changing the earlier view.
  3. Scientists are often wrong.
  4. Studies are expensive.
Show answer

Answer: B. When a passage presents an old belief and new evidence, the main idea is the updated view the evidence supports.
