Words in context

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Reading and Writing · Lesson 2 of 6 · 7 min read

These questions give a sentence with a blank and ask for the most logical and precise word. The test is not about hard vocabulary for its own sake. It is about reading the clues around the blank.

The method

  1. Cover the choices. Read the passage and predict your own word for the blank, even a simple one like "careful" or "not enough."
  2. Find the clue. There is always a phrase that defines the blank: an example after a colon, a contrast word like although or but, or a cause and effect.
  3. Match. Pick the choice closest to your prediction. Eliminate choices with the wrong charge (positive versus negative) first.
Example

"The novelist's prose is notably ______: she rarely uses more words than necessary."

The colon explains the blank: using few words. Predict "short" or "efficient." The answer is economical, not ornate or verbose.

Contrast words (although, but, however, despite, unlike) flip the meaning. Continuation words (and, because, so, in fact, a colon) keep it.
Tip If you do not know a choice, don't pick it just because it sounds impressive. Choose a word you know fits before an unknown word.

Check your understanding

"Although the first report was praised, the second was considered ______, offering few details." Which word fits?

  1. exhaustive
  2. cursory
  3. meticulous
  4. celebrated
Show answer

Answer: B. "Although" contrasts with praise, and "few details" defines the blank: hasty and shallow, or "cursory."
