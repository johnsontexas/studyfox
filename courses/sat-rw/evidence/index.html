Command of evidence: text and data

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Reading and Writing · Lesson 4 of 6 · 8 min read

These questions give you a claim and ask which finding, quotation, or data point supports (or weakens) it.

Text evidence

  1. Write the claim down in a few words. For example: "urban foxes are bolder."
  2. Ask what would prove exactly that. A finding about foxes' diet does not prove boldness, even if it is about urban foxes.
  3. For quotation questions about a poem or novel, the right quote must show every part of the claim. If the claim is "the tone shifts from despair to hope," the quote needs both.

Data evidence (tables and graphs)

  1. Read the title, axis labels, and units first.
  2. Read the claim, then check each choice against the data. Wrong choices often state true numbers that don't address the claim, or numbers that are slightly off.
  3. If the claim has two parts (for example "rose in A while it fell in B"), the answer must address both.
Support means "if this were true, the claim would be more likely." Weaken means the claim would be less likely. A choice about a different topic does neither.

Check your understanding

Claim: "Reading aloud to children helps them learn words." Which finding most directly supports it?

  1. Many children's books rhyme.
  2. Children read to daily knew more words at age four than children rarely read to.
  3. Parents prefer reading at bedtime.
  4. Libraries have story hours.
Show answer

Answer: B. Only this finding connects reading aloud to the outcome in the claim: knowing more words.
