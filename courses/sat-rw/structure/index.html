Text structure, purpose, and two texts

SAT Reading and Writing · Lesson 5 of 6 · 7 min read

Structure and purpose

Purpose questions ask why the author wrote the passage; structure questions ask how it is organized. Describe each sentence's job in a few words: "old belief," "evidence against it," "new conclusion." Then pick the choice that matches that outline.

Tip Check the verb in each choice: argue, describe, explain, criticize, compare. If the passage never argues, an "argue" choice is wrong no matter what follows.

Function of a sentence

When a question asks about one underlined sentence, look at what comes right before and after it. Common jobs: introducing an example, connecting a detail to a conclusion, or presenting a counterargument.

Cross-text connections

Two short texts discuss the same topic. Most questions ask how the author of Text 2 would respond to Text 1.

  1. Summarize each text in one line.
  2. Decide the relationship: agrees, disagrees, or partly agrees (accepts the facts but rejects the conclusion). Partial agreement is the most common correct answer.
IncorrectChoices that make Text 2 more extreme than it is ("denies that any drought occurred").

Check your understanding

Text 1 says a drought caused a city's decline. Text 2 agrees there was a drought but says flooding and conflict also mattered. How would Text 2 respond to Text 1?

  1. By denying the drought happened
  2. By accepting the drought but rejecting it as the only cause
  3. By agreeing completely
  4. By saying the city never declined
Show answer

Answer: B. Text 2 accepts the drought but argues it was one of several causes: partial agreement.
