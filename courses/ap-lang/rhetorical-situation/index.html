The rhetorical situation

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP English Language and Composition · Lesson 2 of 6 · 6 min read

Every text is a response to a situation. Before analyzing, identify:

  • Exigence: the event or problem that prompted the writing.
  • Speaker: who is writing, and what gives them credibility.
  • Audience: who the writer is trying to reach, and what they believe.
  • Purpose: what the writer wants the audience to think, feel, or do.
  • Context: the time, place, and circumstances.

Appeals are tools for a purpose: ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion and values), and logos (reasoning and evidence).

Tip Avoid essays that just label appeals ("this is pathos"). Explain what the choice does for this audience and this purpose.

Check your understanding

What is the "exigence" of a speech?

  1. Its main emotional appeal
  2. The event or problem that prompted it
  3. Its intended audience
  4. Its conclusion
Show answer

Answer: B. Exigence is the urgent occasion that called for the writing, like a tragedy, a policy debate, or a graduation.
