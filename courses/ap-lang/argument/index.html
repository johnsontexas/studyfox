Writing the argument essay

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP English Language and Composition · Lesson 4 of 6 · 7 min read

You get a claim or issue and write your own argument, using evidence from your reading, history, current events, and experience.

  • Take a clear, qualified position. Nuance often earns the sophistication point: "Competition motivates people, but only when the stakes don't crush the joy of the work."
  • Use specific evidence. Named examples (a historical event, a study, a book) beat vague ones ("many people say...").
  • Explain the link between each example and your claim. The example doesn't speak for itself.
  • Address a counterargument, then answer it.
Tip Build an "evidence bank" before the exam: 8 to 10 examples from history, science, literature, and current events that you know well enough to use on almost any topic.

Which piece of evidence is strongest in an argument essay?

  1. "Everyone knows this is true."
  2. "Studies show it."
  3. A specific named example explained in detail
  4. A dictionary definition
Show answer

Answer: C. Specific, well-explained examples are far more convincing and earn more evidence points.
