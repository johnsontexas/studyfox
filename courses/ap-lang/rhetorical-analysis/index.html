Writing the rhetorical analysis essay

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP English Language and Composition · Lesson 3 of 6 · 8 min read

You get a passage (often a speech or letter) and explain how the writer's choices achieve the purpose.

  1. Read for purpose first. What does the writer want, from whom?
  2. Find 2 or 3 major choices, often in the order they appear: an anecdote, a contrast, a shift in tone, repetition, an appeal to shared values.
  3. Thesis: name the purpose and the choices. "To persuade graduates to embrace failure, Rowling shares her own setbacks and redefines success."
  4. Body paragraphs: quote the choice, then explain how it affects the audience and serves the purpose.
IncorrectObama uses pathos in this paragraph to make the audience feel emotion.
CorrectBy describing the firefighters who "ran toward the danger," Obama reminds a grieving nation of its own capacity for courage, shifting the audience from mourning toward resolve.
Use strong verbs for the writer's moves: juxtaposes, concedes, reframes, undercuts, invokes, escalates. They make your commentary precise.

Check your understanding

What should your commentary in a rhetorical analysis focus on?

  1. Whether you agree with the writer
  2. How the writer's choices affect the audience and serve the purpose
  3. Defining every rhetorical device
  4. Summarizing the passage
Show answer

Answer: B. Rhetorical analysis explains how choices work, not whether the argument is right.
