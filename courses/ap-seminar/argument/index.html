Writing and presenting an argument

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Seminar and AP Research · Lesson 4 of 4 · 6 min read

  • Line of reasoning: a clear path from your claim through each reason to your conclusion.
  • Synthesize sources: show where they agree, disagree, and how they build on each other.
  • Consider perspectives: economic, ethical, scientific, cultural, historical, and more.
  • Cite consistently in one style (APA or MLA).
  • Presentations: fewer words on slides, a clear structure, and practice answering questions about your choices and limitations.
Plagiarism, including using someone else's ideas without credit, results in a score of 0 on a Capstone performance task.

What does it mean to "synthesize" sources?

  1. Summarize each one separately
  2. Combine and connect them to build your own argument
  3. Copy their conclusions
  4. Use only one source
Show answer

Answer: B. Synthesis puts sources in conversation with each other and uses them to support a new argument.
