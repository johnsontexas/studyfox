Electric circuits

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics 2 · Lesson 3 of 7 · 9 min read

  • Current: $I = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t}$. Ohm's law: $\Delta V = IR$. Resistance: $R = \frac{\rho L}{A}$.
  • Series: same current through each; $R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + \ldots$
  • Parallel: same voltage across each; $\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \ldots$ Adding a parallel branch lowers total resistance.
  • Kirchhoff's rules: the junction rule (current in equals current out, conservation of charge) and the loop rule (voltages around a loop sum to zero, conservation of energy).
  • Power: $P = I\Delta V = I^2R = \frac{\Delta V^2}{R}$. Brighter bulbs dissipate more power.
  • RC circuits: a charging capacitor eventually blocks current; time constant $\tau = RC$.
Example

Two 6 Ω resistors in parallel: $\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6}$, so $R = 3\ \Omega$. With a 12 V battery, total current is 4 A.

Check your understanding

Adding another resistor in parallel to a circuit does what to the total resistance?

  1. Increases it
  2. Decreases it
  3. Doesn't change it
  4. Makes it zero
Show answer

Answer: B. Each new parallel path gives current another route, so the equivalent resistance goes down.
