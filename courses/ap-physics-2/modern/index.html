AP Physics 2 · Lesson 7 of 7 · 8 min read

  • Photons: $E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$. Light has particle behavior.
  • Photoelectric effect: light ejects electrons only above a threshold frequency, whatever the intensity. $K_{max} = hf - \phi$ (work function). Brighter light ejects more electrons, but not faster ones.
  • Matter waves: de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$. Electron diffraction shows particles act as waves.
  • Atomic energy levels: electrons emit photons when dropping levels; the photon energy equals the energy difference, giving line spectra.
  • Nuclear physics: alpha, beta, and gamma decay; half-life; $E = mc^2$ explains the energy from fission and fusion (mass defect).

In the photoelectric effect, increasing light intensity (above threshold frequency) increases:

  1. The maximum kinetic energy of electrons
  2. The number of electrons ejected
  3. The work function
  4. The threshold frequency
Show answer

Answer: B. More photons eject more electrons, but each photon still has the same energy, so the maximum kinetic energy is unchanged.
