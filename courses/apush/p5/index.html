Period 5: 1844 to 1877

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 6 of 10 · 9 min read

Expansion and the slavery question

  • Manifest Destiny: the belief that the U.S. was destined to spread across the continent. Texas annexation (1845), Oregon (1846), and the Mexican-American War (1846 to 1848) added the Southwest.
  • The Wilmot Proviso (failed) tried to ban slavery in land taken from Mexico.
  • Compromise of 1850: California free; a stronger Fugitive Slave Act angered Northerners. Uncle Tom's Cabin (1852) spread antislavery feeling.
  • Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854): popular sovereignty, leading to "Bleeding Kansas." The Republican Party formed to stop slavery's expansion.
  • Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857): enslaved people were not citizens, and Congress could not ban slavery in territories.
  • John Brown's raid (1859) and Lincoln's election (1860) triggered Southern secession.

Civil War (1861 to 1865)

The North had more people, factories, and railroads. The Emancipation Proclamation (1863) made ending slavery a war aim and kept Britain and France out. About 180,000 Black soldiers served in the Union army. Gettysburg and Vicksburg (1863) turned the war.

Reconstruction (1865 to 1877)

  • The 13th (abolished slavery), 14th (citizenship and equal protection), and 15th (voting regardless of race) Amendments.
  • The Freedmen's Bureau, Black officeholders, and new schools and churches. But Black Codes, the Ku Klux Klan, and sharecropping limited freedom.
  • The Compromise of 1877 removed federal troops from the South, ending Reconstruction.

What was the main issue that the Compromise of 1850 and the Kansas-Nebraska Act tried to settle?

  1. Tariff rates
  2. Whether slavery could expand into western territories
  3. Relations with Native Americans
  4. The national bank

Answer: B. Both tried to resolve whether new territories would allow slavery, and both made sectional conflict worse.
