Period 3: 1754 to 1800

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 4 of 10 · 9 min read

From war to revolution

  • French and Indian War (1754 to 1763): Britain won North America east of the Mississippi but went deep into debt.
  • Britain ended salutary neglect: the Proclamation of 1763 (no settlement west of the Appalachians), the Sugar Act, Stamp Act (1765), Townshend Acts, and the Coercive (Intolerable) Acts.
  • Colonists protested "no taxation without representation" through boycotts, the Stamp Act Congress, and the Sons of Liberty.
  • Thomas Paine's Common Sense (1776) and the Declaration of Independence drew on Enlightenment ideas of natural rights.
  • The Patriots won with French help after Saratoga (1777); the war ended at Yorktown (1781) and the Treaty of Paris (1783).

Building a government

  • Articles of Confederation: a weak central government with no power to tax or regulate trade. Success: the Northwest Ordinance (1787). Failure: Shays' Rebellion (1786) exposed its weakness.
  • Constitutional Convention (1787): compromises included the Great Compromise (two houses of Congress) and the Three-Fifths Compromise.
  • Federalists vs. Anti-Federalists: the Federalist Papers argued for ratification; Anti-Federalists demanded a Bill of Rights (1791).
  • Washington's presidency: Hamilton's financial plan, the Whiskey Rebellion, and the Farewell Address warning against parties and foreign alliances. The first parties: Federalists and Democratic-Republicans.
Causation chain to know: war debt led to new taxes, which led to protest, then revolution, then a weak government, then the Constitution.
