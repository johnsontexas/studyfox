Period 4: 1800 to 1848

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 5 of 10 · 9 min read

Politics

  • Jefferson (1800): the Louisiana Purchase (1803) doubled the country's size, even though Jefferson doubted the Constitution allowed it.
  • Marbury v. Madison (1803) established judicial review; the Marshall Court strengthened federal power (McCulloch v. Maryland, Gibbons v. Ogden).
  • War of 1812 boosted nationalism and ended the Federalist Party. The Era of Good Feelings followed.
  • Missouri Compromise (1820): Missouri a slave state, Maine free, no slavery north of 36°30'.
  • Monroe Doctrine (1823): Europe should stay out of the Western Hemisphere.
  • Jacksonian democracy: voting expanded to nearly all white men; the spoils system; the Bank War; the Nullification Crisis; the Indian Removal Act (1830) and the Trail of Tears. The Whig Party formed in opposition.

The Market Revolution

Canals (the Erie Canal), steamboats, and railroads linked regions. Factories grew in the North (the Lowell system), the cotton gin expanded slavery in the South, and Henry Clay's American System proposed a tariff, a national bank, and internal improvements.

Reform and religion

The Second Great Awakening inspired reform movements: temperance, abolition (William Lloyd Garrison, Frederick Douglass), women's rights (the Seneca Falls Convention, 1848), public schools (Horace Mann), and asylum reform (Dorothea Dix).
