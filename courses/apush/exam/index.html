The AP U.S. History exam: format and scoring

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 1 of 10 · 7 min read

The AP U.S. History exam is 3 hours 15 minutes and has four parts:

PartQuestionsTimeWeight
Multiple choice55, in sets of 3 or 4 tied to a source55 min40%
Short answer (SAQ)3 (questions 1 and 2, then a choice of 3 or 4)40 min20%
Document-based question (DBQ)1 essay with 7 documents60 min (includes 15 min reading)25%
Long essay (LEQ)1, chosen from 3 options40 min15%

The DBQ rubric (7 points)

  • Thesis (1): a historically defensible claim with a line of reasoning, in the intro or conclusion.
  • Contextualization (1): a few sentences on the broader events before or during the prompt's period.
  • Evidence (3): use the content of at least 4 documents to support your argument (2 points), plus one specific piece of evidence that is not in the documents (1 point).
  • Analysis and reasoning (2): explain how or why the point of view, purpose, historical situation, or audience matters for at least 2 documents (1 point), and show complex understanding (1 point), for example by weighing multiple causes or explaining both similarity and difference.

The LEQ rubric (6 points)

Thesis (1), contextualization (1), evidence (2: specific examples, then examples that support the argument), and analysis and reasoning (2: use a historical reasoning skill, then show complex understanding).

SAQs

Each SAQ has parts a, b, and c. Answer each part in two or three sentences: answer, explain, give a specific example. No thesis or introduction needed.

The three reasoning skills the essays test are causation, comparison, and continuity and change over time. Identify which one the prompt wants before you write.
Tip Contextualization is the easiest point to lose. Write it first, as your opening, so you never forget it.

In a DBQ, which earns the sourcing point?

  1. Quoting a document at length
  2. Explaining how the author's purpose shapes what a document says
  3. Listing all seven documents in order
  4. Summarizing each document in your own words
Show answer

Answer: B. Sourcing means explaining why point of view, purpose, historical situation, or audience matters to the argument, for at least two documents.
