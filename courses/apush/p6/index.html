Period 6: 1865 to 1898

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 7 of 10 · 8 min read

The Gilded Age economy

  • Industrialization boomed: steel (Carnegie), oil (Rockefeller), railroads, banking (J.P. Morgan). Big business used vertical and horizontal integration, trusts, and monopolies.
  • Justifications: Social Darwinism and Carnegie's Gospel of Wealth (the rich should give back through philanthropy).
  • Labor: long hours, low pay, and dangerous conditions. The Knights of Labor and the American Federation of Labor (AFL). Violent strikes: the Great Railroad Strike (1877), Haymarket (1886), Homestead (1892), Pullman (1894).

Society

  • New immigration from Southern and Eastern Europe, and from China until the Chinese Exclusion Act (1882).
  • Cities grew fast; political machines (Tammany Hall) traded services for votes. Settlement houses (Jane Addams's Hull House) helped immigrants.
  • Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) upheld "separate but equal." Jim Crow laws and voting restrictions spread in the South.

The West

The Homestead Act, transcontinental railroad (1869), and the slaughter of the buffalo transformed the West. Wars and the reservation system pushed Native Americans off their land; the Dawes Act (1887) tried to force assimilation by breaking up tribal land. Wounded Knee (1890) marked the end of armed resistance.

Populism

Struggling farmers formed the Grange and the Populist (People's) Party (1892), calling for free silver, a graduated income tax, and government ownership of railroads.

What was the goal of the Dawes Act (1887)?

  1. To protect tribal landholdings
  2. To assimilate Native Americans by dividing tribal land into individual plots
  3. To create the first national parks
  4. To end the buffalo hunt
Show answer

Answer: B. It broke up communal reservation land into individual allotments, aiming to turn Native Americans into individual farmers. Tribes lost most of their land as a result.
