Period 9: 1980 to the present

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 10 of 10 · 7 min read

  • The conservative resurgence: Ronald Reagan (1980) cut taxes and regulation, increased defense spending, and appealed to the New Right and religious conservatives. "Reaganomics" relied on supply-side economics.
  • The end of the Cold War: Gorbachev's reforms, the fall of the Berlin Wall (1989), and the collapse of the USSR (1991).
  • New role in the world: the Gulf War (1991), interventions in the Balkans, and after the September 11, 2001 attacks, the War on Terror, the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, and debates over the Patriot Act and civil liberties.
  • Economy: the shift from manufacturing to services and technology, globalization (NAFTA, 1994), the internet, growing income inequality, and the 2008 financial crisis.
  • Society: immigration (especially from Latin America and Asia) changed demographics; debates over immigration, the environment, and the role of government continued.
Period 9 is lightly tested (about 5% of the exam). Focus on Reagan, the end of the Cold War, globalization, and 9/11.
