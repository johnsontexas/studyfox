Period 1: 1491 to 1607

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 2 of 10 · 7 min read

Before Europeans arrived, Native American societies were diverse and adapted to their environments.

  • Southwest: maize cultivation spread north from Mexico, supporting settled societies like the Pueblo.
  • Great Plains and Great Basin: many groups were mobile hunter-gatherers.
  • Northeast and Mississippi Valley: a mix of farming, hunting, and permanent villages.
  • Northwest and California: rich in fish and game, supporting permanent villages without much farming.

Contact and the Columbian Exchange

Columbus's 1492 voyage started the Columbian Exchange: crops (maize and potatoes to Europe; wheat and sugar to the Americas), animals (horses, cattle, pigs), and diseases. Smallpox and other diseases killed a huge share of Native people, who had no immunity.

Spanish colonization

  • Encomienda system: Spanish colonists were granted Native labor, which led to brutal exploitation.
  • Casta system: a social hierarchy based on mixed European, African, and Native ancestry.
  • Valladolid debate (1550): Bartolomé de Las Casas argued against the abuse of Native people; Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda defended it.
  • As Native populations fell, the Spanish and Portuguese turned to enslaved Africans.
Big theme: contact transformed both worlds. Explain changes on the European, African, and Native American sides.

Check your understanding

Which best explains the sharp decline of Native American populations after 1492?

  1. Large-scale warfare among Native groups
  2. Diseases like smallpox to which Native people had no immunity
  3. Mass migration to Europe
  4. Crop failures caused by maize
Show answer

Answer: B. Epidemic disease was the main cause of population collapse, though violence and forced labor added to it.
