Period 2: 1607 to 1754

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. History · Lesson 3 of 10 · 8 min read

Different European colonial goals

  • Spain: conquest, converting Native people, and extracting wealth.
  • France and the Netherlands: trade (especially fur), with fewer settlers and more alliances and intermarriage with Native peoples.
  • England: large numbers of settlers, including families, who wanted land, which led to conflict with Native peoples.

The British colonial regions

  • Chesapeake (Virginia, Maryland): tobacco, indentured servants and then enslaved Africans. Jamestown (1607). Bacon's Rebellion (1676) pushed planters toward enslaved labor instead of indentured servants.
  • New England: Puritan families, towns, farming, fishing, shipbuilding. Town meetings and the Mayflower Compact show early self-government.
  • Middle colonies: diverse (Quakers in Pennsylvania), grain exports, relatively tolerant.
  • Southern colonies and West Indies: rice, indigo, and sugar plantations with large enslaved majorities.

Key developments

  • Atlantic trade linked the colonies, Britain, Africa, and the Caribbean. Mercantilism and the Navigation Acts tried to make colonies serve Britain.
  • Salutary neglect: Britain loosely enforced its rules, so colonies developed self-government (the House of Burgesses, colonial assemblies).
  • The Great Awakening (1730s to 1740s) and the Enlightenment both encouraged questioning authority.
  • Native resistance: Pueblo Revolt (1680), King Philip's (Metacom's) War (1675).
  • Slavery became hereditary and race-based through colonial law codes. The Stono Rebellion (1739) was a major revolt.

Check your understanding

What was one major result of Bacon's Rebellion (1676)?

  1. Virginia abolished slavery
  2. Planters relied more on enslaved Africans than on indentured servants
  3. England granted Virginia independence
  4. Puritans took control of Virginia
Show answer

Answer: B. The uprising of poor former servants alarmed elites, who turned to enslaved labor, a permanent and more controllable workforce.
