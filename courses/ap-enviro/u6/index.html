Unit 6: Energy Resources and Consumption

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Environmental Science · Lesson 6 of 9 · 8 min read

  • Nonrenewable: coal (cheap, highest CO₂), oil, natural gas (cleanest fossil fuel, but methane leaks), and nuclear (no CO₂, but radioactive waste and accident risk).
  • Fracking releases natural gas from shale; concerns include groundwater contamination and earthquakes.
  • Renewable: solar, wind, hydroelectric (disrupts rivers), geothermal, biomass (can be carbon neutral but causes air pollution), and hydrogen fuel cells.
  • Electricity generation: most plants use heat to make steam that turns a turbine connected to a generator. Fossil fuel plants are about 33% efficient.
  • Conservation: efficiency standards, insulation, LED lights, and public transit.
Energy calculation

A 100 W bulb runs 5 hours a day for 30 days: $100 \times 5 \times 30 = 15{,}000$ Wh = 15 kWh. At 12 cents per kWh, that costs 1.80 dollars.

Tip Calculators are allowed, but APES calculation questions still require you to show your setup and units. Write out the dimensional analysis step by step.

Check your understanding

Which fossil fuel releases the least CO₂ per unit of energy when burned?

  1. Coal
  2. Oil
  3. Natural gas
  4. They are all the same
Show answer

Answer: C. Natural gas (methane) has the highest ratio of hydrogen to carbon, so it releases the least CO₂ per unit of energy.
