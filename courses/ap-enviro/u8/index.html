Unit 8: Aquatic and Terrestrial Pollution

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Environmental Science · Lesson 8 of 9 · 7 min read

  • Point sources (a single pipe) vs. nonpoint sources (runoff from many farms; harder to regulate).
  • Eutrophication: excess nitrogen and phosphorus cause algal blooms. When the algae die, decomposers use up oxygen, creating dead zones.
  • Thermal pollution lowers dissolved oxygen.
  • Bioaccumulation (builds up in one organism) and biomagnification (increases up the food chain): mercury, DDT.
  • Solid waste: landfills (leachate, methane), incineration, recycling, and composting. Ocean plastics break into microplastics.
  • Sewage treatment: primary (physical settling), secondary (bacteria break down organic matter), and tertiary (removes nutrients).
  • Dose-response curves: LD50 is the dose that kills half the test population.
