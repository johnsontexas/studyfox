Unit 7: Atmospheric Pollution

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Environmental Science · Lesson 7 of 9 · 7 min read

  • Primary pollutants are emitted directly (CO, SO₂, NOₓ, particulates). Secondary pollutants form in the air (ground-level ozone, acid rain).
  • Photochemical smog: NOₓ and VOCs react in sunlight to form ozone. Worst on hot, sunny days in cities with traffic.
  • Thermal inversions trap pollution near the ground.
  • Acid rain comes from SO₂ and NOₓ; it harms lakes and forests. Limestone can neutralize it.
  • Indoor pollution: radon, carbon monoxide, asbestos, and smoke from burning biomass.
  • Controls: the Clean Air Act, catalytic converters, scrubbers, and electrostatic precipitators.
  • Noise pollution also harms wildlife and people.

Ground-level ozone is a secondary pollutant because:

  1. It is emitted directly by cars
  2. It forms when NOₓ and VOCs react in sunlight
  3. It comes from volcanoes
  4. It only exists in the stratosphere
Show answer

Answer: B. It isn't released directly; it forms from reactions among primary pollutants in sunlight.
