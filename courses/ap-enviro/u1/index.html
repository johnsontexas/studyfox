Unit 1: The Living World: Ecosystems

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Environmental Science · Lesson 1 of 9 · 7 min read

The exam: 80 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 60%) and 3 free-response questions (70 min, 40%): design an investigation, analyze an environmental problem and propose a solution, and the same with calculations. Calculators are allowed.

  • Species interactions: predation, competition, mutualism, commensalism, parasitism. Resource partitioning reduces competition.
  • Biomes are defined by temperature and precipitation (tundra, taiga, temperate forest, grassland, desert, tropical rainforest). Aquatic biomes depend on salinity, depth, and flow.
  • Cycles: carbon (fastest through photosynthesis and respiration; longest storage in rocks and fossil fuels), nitrogen (fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification, denitrification), phosphorus (no gas phase; limiting nutrient), and water.
  • Energy flow: the 10% rule. Primary productivity: NPP = GPP − respiration.

Why is phosphorus often a limiting nutrient?

  1. It is a gas that escapes easily
  2. Its cycle has no atmospheric phase and weathering from rock is slow
  3. Plants don't need it
  4. It is produced by lightning
Show answer

Answer: B. Phosphorus moves mainly through rock weathering, which is slow, so ecosystems often have little available.
