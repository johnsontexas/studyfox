Unit 6: Consequences of Industrialization (1750 to 1900)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 7 of 10 · 8 min read

The new imperialism

Industrial states needed raw materials and markets, and they had new tools: steamships, the telegraph, quinine (against malaria), and machine guns.

  • Africa: the Berlin Conference (1884 to 1885) divided Africa among European powers with no Africans present.
  • Asia: Britain ruled India directly after 1857; the Opium Wars forced China to open ports (unequal treaties, spheres of influence).
  • Justifications: Social Darwinism and the "civilizing mission" (the "White Man's Burden").

Resistance

The Sepoy Rebellion (1857) in India, the Boxer Rebellion (1900) in China, the Xhosa Cattle Killing movement, and Ethiopia's victory over Italy at Adwa (1896).

Global economy and migration

  • Colonies became exporters of cash crops and raw materials (cotton, rubber, palm oil), creating economic dependence.
  • Migration: millions moved to cities and abroad, including indentured laborers from India and China working in the Caribbean, Africa, and the Pacific.
  • Reactions to migrants: the Chinese Exclusion Act (U.S.) and the White Australia policy.
