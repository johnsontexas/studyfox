Unit 9: Globalization (1900 to present)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 10 of 10 · 7 min read

  • Technology: radio, air travel, the internet, and cell phones made the world far more connected. The Green Revolution raised crop yields with new seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation, though it favored wealthier farmers.
  • Medicine: vaccines and antibiotics cut death rates (smallpox was eradicated), but HIV/AIDS, malaria, and new pandemics remain.
  • Environment: population growth, fossil fuels, and deforestation drive climate change and pollution.
  • Economy: free-market policies spread (Thatcher, Reagan, Deng Xiaoping's reforms in China). Free trade agreements and organizations like the WTO and NAFTA. Multinational corporations.
  • Rights movements: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948), women's rights, civil rights, and the anti-apartheid movement.
  • Global culture: sports, music, and media spread worldwide (Bollywood, K-pop, reggae). Some groups resisted globalization to protect local cultures or jobs.
  • Institutions: the United Nations, the European Union, ASEAN, and the African Union.
