Unit 1: The Global Tapestry (1200 to 1450)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 2 of 10 · 8 min read

This unit surveys how states developed and kept power in different regions around 1200.

  • East Asia: the Song Dynasty used Confucianism and the civil service exam to run a large bureaucracy. Champa rice, the Grand Canal, and commercialization grew the economy. Neo-Confucianism spread to Korea, Japan, and Vietnam.
  • Dar al-Islam: the Abbasid Caliphate declined and new Muslim states rose (the Seljuks, Mamluks, Delhi Sultanate). Muslim scholars advanced math, medicine, and preserved Greek texts.
  • South and Southeast Asia: Hinduism and Buddhism shaped states like the Vijayanagara Empire, the Khmer Empire (Angkor Wat), and the sea-based Srivijaya Empire. Majapahit controlled sea trade.
  • The Americas: the Aztec (Mexica) used a tribute system; the Inca used the mit'a labor system and roads. Earlier, Cahokia and the Maya city-states.
  • Africa: Mali grew rich on gold and trans-Saharan trade (Mansa Musa); the Swahili city-states traded across the Indian Ocean; Great Zimbabwe; Ethiopia was Christian.
  • Europe: decentralized feudalism and manorialism; the Catholic Church was the main unifying force.
Tested skill: comparing how states gained and kept power. Common methods are religion, bureaucracy, tribute, military force, and trade.
