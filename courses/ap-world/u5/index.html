Unit 5: Revolutions (1750 to 1900)

Enlightenment and political revolutions

Enlightenment thinkers (Locke's natural rights, Rousseau's social contract, Montesquieu's separation of powers) challenged absolute monarchy and inspired revolutions:

  • American Revolution (1776): independence and a republic.
  • French Revolution (1789): ended the monarchy, issued the Declaration of the Rights of Man, then the Terror and Napoleon.
  • Haitian Revolution (1791 to 1804): the only successful revolt by enslaved people that founded a state, led by Toussaint Louverture.
  • Latin American independence (1810s to 1820s): led by creoles like Simón Bolívar.

Nationalism unified Italy and Germany (Bismarck) and fueled independence movements.

The Industrial Revolution

  • Began in Britain because of coal and iron, rivers and ports, capital from trade, and an agricultural revolution that freed workers.
  • The factory system, steam engine, and later the Second Industrial Revolution (steel, chemicals, electricity).
  • Spread to Western Europe, the U.S., Russia, and Japan (the Meiji Restoration, state-led industrialization).

Responses

Workers formed unions; Marx and Engels wrote the Communist Manifesto (1848). Reforms like expanded voting and factory laws followed.
