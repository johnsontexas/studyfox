Unit 7: Global Conflict (1900 to present)

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 8 of 10 · 9 min read

World War I (1914 to 1918)

Causes: MAIN: militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism, plus the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. It was a total war: governments directed whole economies, used propaganda, and recruited colonial soldiers. The war destroyed four empires (Russian, German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman).

Between the wars

  • The Russian Revolution (1917) created the first communist state; Stalin later forced collectivization and five-year plans.
  • The Treaty of Versailles punished Germany and created the League of Nations.
  • The Great Depression led governments to intervene in economies (the New Deal) and helped fascists rise (Mussolini, Hitler).
  • Japan expanded into Manchuria (1931) and China (1937).

World War II (1939 to 1945)

Allied (Britain, the USSR, the U.S., China) vs. Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan). Total war again, with mass bombing of cities and the atomic bomb. The Holocaust killed six million Jews and millions of others. Other mass atrocities in the century include the Armenian genocide, the Holodomor, and the Rwandan genocide.
