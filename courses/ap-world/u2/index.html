Unit 2: Networks of Exchange (1200 to 1450)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 3 of 10 · 8 min read

The trade routes

  • Silk Roads: luxury goods (silk, porcelain) moved overland. Caravanserai, paper money (flying cash), and banking made trade easier.
  • Indian Ocean: the largest network, powered by the monsoon winds. Goods included spices, cotton, and porcelain. Merchant communities (diasporas) settled in port cities like Calicut and Malacca.
  • Trans-Saharan: camels and camel saddles made crossing the Sahara possible. Gold and salt were key goods, and the trade made Mali powerful.

The Mongols

The Mongol Empire, the largest land empire in history, conquered much of Eurasia under Chinggis Khan and his successors. They taxed and protected Silk Road trade (the "Pax Mongolica"), which spread technology and ideas, but also helped spread the Black Death in the 1300s.

Effects of exchange

  • Cultural diffusion: Islam spread through merchants into West Africa and Southeast Asia; Buddhism spread across Asia.
  • Travelers like Ibn Battuta and Marco Polo recorded the connected world.
  • Technology and crops: gunpowder and paper moved west; Champa rice spread; sugar and citrus spread through the Muslim world.

  3. The Mongol postal relay system
  4. Steam-powered ships
