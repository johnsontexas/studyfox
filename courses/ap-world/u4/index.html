Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections (1450 to 1750)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 5 of 10 · 9 min read

Why exploration took off

New technology (the caravel, the astrolabe, magnetic compass, better maps) and state support, like Portugal's Prince Henry, drove European exploration. Motives: gold, God, and glory, plus a sea route to Asian spices.

Major effects

  • The Columbian Exchange: disease devastated the Americas; new crops (potatoes, maize) fed population growth in Afro-Eurasia.
  • Maritime empires: Portugal built a trading-post empire in the Indian Ocean; Spain conquered the Aztec and Inca; the Dutch and British used joint-stock companies (the Dutch East India Company, the British East India Company).
  • Silver: mined at Potosí with mit'a labor, silver flowed to China, which required taxes in silver. It made trade truly global.
  • Labor systems: the Atlantic slave trade moved about 12 million enslaved Africans; the encomienda and hacienda systems; indentured servitude.
  • Social hierarchies: the casta system in Spanish America.

Resistance

The Pueblo Revolt, maroon communities of escaped enslaved people, and the Ana Nzinga resistance against the Portuguese in Angola.

  3. Silver was used to build the Great Wall

Answer: B. Ming China's shift to silver-based taxes created huge demand, and Spanish silver from Potosí flowed there through Manila and Europe.
