Unit 3: Land-Based Empires (1450 to 1750)

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP World History: Modern · Lesson 4 of 10 · 8 min read

The gunpowder empires used cannons and firearms to expand and hold large territories.

  • Ottoman Empire (Sunni): took Constantinople in 1453. Used the devshirme system to recruit Christian boys as janissaries (soldiers) and officials.
  • Safavid Empire (Shia, Persia): its conflict with the Ottomans deepened the Sunni-Shia split.
  • Mughal Empire (India): Muslim rulers over a Hindu majority. Akbar practiced religious tolerance; later Aurangzeb did not. Built the Taj Mahal.
  • Qing Dynasty (China): Manchu rulers kept Confucian bureaucracy and limited foreign trade.
  • Tokugawa Japan: centralized power under the shogun and isolated Japan from most foreign contact.

Ways rulers legitimized and consolidated power

  • Monumental architecture (Versailles, the Taj Mahal), religion (divine right in Europe), and bureaucracy, including tax farming (Mughal zamindars, Ottoman tax farmers).

Religion in Europe

The Protestant Reformation (Luther, 1517) split Western Christianity and led to religious wars. The Catholic Church responded with the Counter-Reformation.
