Using derivatives: max, min, and graph shape

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Calculus · Lesson 4 of 6 · 8 min read

  • Critical points are where $f'(x) = 0$ or $f'$ is undefined.
  • If $f'$ changes from $+$ to $-$, there's a local maximum; from $-$ to $+$, a local minimum.
  • $f'' > 0$: concave up (cup shape). $f'' < 0$: concave down. Where concavity changes is an inflection point.
Example

$f(x) = x^3 - 3x$. Then $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 0$ at $x = \pm 1$. $f(-1) = 2$ is a local max, and $f(1) = -2$ is a local min.

Optimization

  1. Write a formula for the quantity to maximize or minimize.
  2. Use the constraint to get it in one variable.
  3. Set the derivative to zero and solve.
  4. Check endpoints and confirm it's a max or min.

Where does $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 1$ have its minimum?

  1. $x = -3$
  2. $x = 1$
  3. $x = 3$
  4. $x = 6$
Show answer

Answer: C. $f'(x) = 2x - 6 = 0$ at $x = 3$, and $f''(x) = 2 > 0$, so it's a minimum.
