What a derivative means

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Calculus · Lesson 2 of 6 · 7 min read

The derivative $f'(x)$ is the instantaneous rate of change of $f$, or the slope of the tangent line at a point.

$f'(x) = \displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}$

This is the slope between two points on the curve, as the points slide together.

Example

For $f(x) = x^2$: $\dfrac{(x + h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \dfrac{2xh + h^2}{h} = 2x + h$. As $h \to 0$, this becomes $f'(x) = 2x$.

Interpreting derivatives

  • If $s(t)$ is position, $s'(t)$ is velocity and $s''(t)$ is acceleration.
  • $f' > 0$ means $f$ is increasing; $f' < 0$ means decreasing.
  • Units of $f'$ are "units of $f$ per unit of $x$," like meters per second.

Check your understanding

A particle's position is $s(t) = t^2$ meters. What is its velocity at $t = 3$ seconds?

  1. $3$ m/s
  2. $6$ m/s
  3. $9$ m/s
  4. $2$ m/s
Show answer

Answer: B. Velocity is the derivative: $s'(t) = 2t$, so $s'(3) = 6$ m/s.
