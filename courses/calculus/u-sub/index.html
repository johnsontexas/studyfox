u-substitution

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Calculus · Lesson 6 of 6 · 6 min read

u-substitution undoes the chain rule. Use it when you see a function and (a multiple of) its derivative in the same integral.

  1. Let $u$ be the "inside" function.
  2. Find $du$ and rewrite the integral entirely in $u$.
  3. Integrate, then substitute back.
Example

$\displaystyle\int 2x(x^2 + 1)^3\,dx$. Let $u = x^2 + 1$, so $du = 2x\,dx$. The integral becomes $\int u^3\,du = \frac{u^4}{4} + C = \frac{(x^2 + 1)^4}{4} + C$.

Tip Check any indefinite integral by differentiating your answer. You should get back the original integrand.

Check your understanding

Find $\displaystyle\int \cos(3x)\,dx$.

  1. $\sin(3x) + C$
  2. $3\sin(3x) + C$
  3. $\frac{1}{3}\sin(3x) + C$
  4. $-\frac{1}{3}\sin(3x) + C$
Show answer

Answer: C. Let $u = 3x$, so $dx = \frac{du}{3}$. Then $\frac{1}{3}\int \cos u\,du = \frac{1}{3}\sin(3x) + C$. Differentiating gives back $\cos(3x)$.
