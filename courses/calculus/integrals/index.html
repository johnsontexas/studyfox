Integrals and the Fundamental Theorem

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Calculus · Lesson 5 of 6 · 8 min read

Integration reverses differentiation. An antiderivative of $f$ is a function $F$ with $F' = f$.

Power rule for integrals: $\displaystyle\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$ for $n \ne -1$. And $\displaystyle\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + C$.

Definite integrals

$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is the signed area between the curve and the x-axis from $a$ to $b$. Area below the axis counts as negative.

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$
Example

$\displaystyle\int_0^2 3x^2\,dx = \Big[x^3\Big]_0^2 = 8 - 0 = 8$.

Tip Desmos can check definite integrals: type int to get the integral symbol.

Check your understanding

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^3 2x\,dx$.

  1. $4$
  2. $6$
  3. $8$
  4. $9$
Show answer

Answer: C. An antiderivative of $2x$ is $x^2$. So $3^2 - 1^2 = 9 - 1 = 8$.
