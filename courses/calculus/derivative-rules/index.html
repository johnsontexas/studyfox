Derivative rules

Calculus · Lesson 3 of 6 · 8 min read

RuleFormula
Constant$\frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0$
Power$\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1}$
Product$(fg)' = f'g + fg'$
Quotient$\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$
Chain$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$

Derivatives to memorize

$\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$, $\frac{d}{dx}e^x = e^x$, $\frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \frac{1}{x}$.

Power rule

$\frac{d}{dx}(3x^4 - 5x + 2) = 12x^3 - 5$

Chain rule

$\frac{d}{dx}(2x + 1)^5 = 5(2x + 1)^4 \cdot 2 = 10(2x + 1)^4$. Take the derivative of the outside, keep the inside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

Check your understanding

What is the derivative of $\sin(x^2)$?

  1. $\cos(x^2)$
  2. $2x\cos(x^2)$
  3. $\cos(2x)$
  4. $-2x\cos(x^2)$
Show answer

Answer: B. Chain rule: the outside $\sin$ becomes $\cos(x^2)$, times the derivative of the inside, $2x$.
