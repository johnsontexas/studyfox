Unit 2: Interactions Among the Branches

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. Government and Politics · Lesson 3 of 6 · 10 min read

Congress

  • House: 435 members, 2-year terms, starts revenue bills, impeaches. The Rules Committee and the Speaker control the agenda.
  • Senate: 100 members, 6-year terms, confirms appointments, ratifies treaties, holds impeachment trials. The filibuster can be ended by cloture (60 votes).
  • Members act as delegates (vote as constituents want), trustees (use own judgment), or politicos (a mix). Gerrymandering and divided government shape how Congress works.

The presidency

  • Formal powers: veto, commander in chief, treaties, appointments, pardons.
  • Informal powers: executive orders, executive agreements, signing statements, and the bully pulpit.
  • Federalist No. 70 (Hamilton): a single, energetic executive is needed. The 22nd Amendment limits presidents to two terms.

The courts

  • Marbury v. Madison (1803) established judicial review. Federalist No. 78: the judiciary is the "least dangerous" branch and needs life terms for independence.
  • Judicial activism vs. judicial restraint; precedent (stare decisis).

The bureaucracy

Cabinet departments and agencies carry out laws using discretionary and rule-making authority. Congress checks them through oversight hearings and funding (the "power of the purse"). Iron triangles link agencies, congressional committees, and interest groups.

Check your understanding

Which is an informal power of the president?

  1. Vetoing a bill
  2. Issuing an executive order
  3. Granting a pardon
  4. Making treaties
Show answer

Answer: B. Executive orders are not listed in the Constitution; they come from the president's role in executing the law. The others are formal, listed powers.
