Unit 1: Foundations of American Democracy

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP U.S. Government and Politics · Lesson 2 of 6 · 9 min read

  • Ideas behind the Constitution: natural rights, popular sovereignty, social contract, and republicanism (Locke, Montesquieu).
  • Models of democracy: participatory (broad citizen participation), pluralist (groups compete for influence), and elite (a small, educated group has the most influence).
  • Articles of Confederation: no power to tax, no executive, and amendments needed unanimous consent. Shays' Rebellion showed the weakness.
  • Compromises: the Great Compromise (House by population, Senate equal), the Three-Fifths Compromise, and the Electoral College.
  • Federalist No. 10 (Madison): a large republic controls the dangers of factions. Brutus No. 1: a large republic would be too distant and too powerful.
  • Federalist No. 51: separation of powers and checks and balances. "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition."

Federalism

  • Enumerated powers (listed for Congress), reserved powers (states, 10th Amendment), concurrent powers (shared, like taxing), and implied powers (the Necessary and Proper Clause).
  • McCulloch v. Maryland (1819): Congress has implied powers, and federal law is supreme over state law (the Supremacy Clause).
  • U.S. v. Lopez (1995): Congress overstepped the Commerce Clause by banning guns near schools, a win for state power.
  • Fiscal federalism: categorical grants (strings attached), block grants (broad purposes), and unfunded mandates.
