Required foundational documents (9)

The Declaration of Independence, the Articles of Confederation, the Constitution, Federalist No. 10, 51, 70, and 78, Brutus No. 1, and Martin Luther King Jr.'s Letter from a Birmingham Jail.

Required Supreme Court cases (15)

Marbury v. Madison, McCulloch v. Maryland, Schenck v. U.S., Brown v. Board of Education, Baker v. Carr, Engel v. Vitale, Gideon v. Wainwright, Tinker v. Des Moines, New York Times v. U.S., Wisconsin v. Yoder, Roe v. Wade, Shaw v. Reno, U.S. v. Lopez, McDonald v. Chicago, and Citizens United v. FEC.

The argument essay

Take a position, support it with two pieces of evidence (at least one from the required documents), explain your reasoning, and respond to an opposing view with a rebuttal or concession.

Tip Make flashcards for each case: facts, the constitutional clause involved, the holding, and why it matters. The SCOTUS comparison FRQ asks you to apply a required case to a new one.

In the argument essay, what must you do with an opposing perspective?

  1. Ignore it
  2. Respond to it with a rebuttal, concession, or refutation
  3. Make it your thesis
  4. Only mention it in the conclusion

Answer: B. One rubric point requires you to address an alternative perspective and respond to it.
