How learning actually works

How to Learn Any Subject · Lesson 1 of 7 · 6 min read

Rereading and highlighting feel productive, but research on learning shows they're among the weakest methods. Four habits do most of the work:

  1. Active recall: pull information out of your memory. Close the book and write what you remember, or quiz yourself.
  2. Spaced repetition: review a day later, then a few days later, then a week later.
  3. Interleaving: mix problem types instead of doing 20 of the same kind in a row.
  4. Explaining: teach it to someone (or an imaginary student). Where you get stuck is the gap to fill.
If studying feels easy, you probably aren't learning much. Effortful recall is what builds memory.
