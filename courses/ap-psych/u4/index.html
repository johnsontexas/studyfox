Unit 4: Social Psychology and Personality

AP Psychology · Lesson 5 of 6 · 9 min read

Social psychology

  • Attribution: the fundamental attribution error overemphasizes personality and underestimates the situation when judging others.
  • Cognitive dissonance: discomfort when actions and beliefs conflict, often resolved by changing beliefs.
  • Conformity (Asch's line study), obedience (Milgram's shock study), and the bystander effect.
  • Group effects: group polarization, groupthink, social facilitation, social loafing, and deindividuation.
  • Prejudice: stereotypes, in-group bias, and the just-world phenomenon.

Personality

  • Psychodynamic (Freud: id, ego, superego, defense mechanisms), humanistic (Maslow, Rogers: self-actualization, unconditional positive regard), social-cognitive (Bandura: self-efficacy, reciprocal determinism), and trait theories (the Big Five: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism).

Motivation and emotion

Drive-reduction, arousal theory (Yerkes-Dodson), and Maslow's hierarchy. Emotion theories: James-Lange, Cannon-Bard, and Schachter-Singer two-factor.
