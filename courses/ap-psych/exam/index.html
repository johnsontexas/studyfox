The exam and research methods

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Psychology · Lesson 1 of 6 · 8 min read

The exam: 75 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 66.7%) and 2 free-response questions (70 min, 33.3%): an Article Analysis Question (AAQ) about a research study and an Evidence-Based Question (EBQ) using three sources.

Research methods (tested throughout)

  • Experiment: the only method that shows cause and effect. Random assignment to an experimental and a control group. The independent variable is manipulated; the dependent variable is measured.
  • Correlation: shows a relationship but not causation. Coefficient r runs from −1 to +1.
  • Other methods: case studies, naturalistic observation, surveys (watch for wording effects and sampling bias), and meta-analysis.
  • Random sampling (who is in the study, for generalizing) is different from random assignment (which group they're in, for causation).
  • Ethics: informed consent, protection from harm, confidentiality, and debriefing.
If a question says a study "shows that X causes Y," check the method. Only an experiment supports a causal claim.

Check your understanding

A study finds that students who sleep more get higher grades. What can you conclude?

  1. Sleep causes higher grades
  2. Higher grades cause more sleep
  3. Sleep and grades are related, but causation is not shown
  4. There is no relationship
Show answer

Answer: C. This is correlational. A third variable (like stress or schedule) could explain both.
