Unit 3: National Income and Price Determination

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Macroeconomics · Lesson 4 of 7 · 9 min read

  • Aggregate demand (AD) slopes down (wealth, interest rate, and exchange rate effects). Shifted by changes in C, I, G, or net exports.
  • Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) slopes up. Shifted by input prices (wages, oil), productivity, and expectations.
  • Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) is vertical at full-employment output.
  • Recessionary gap: output below full employment. Inflationary gap: output above it.
  • Multipliers: spending multiplier = 1 ÷ MPS. Tax multiplier = −MPC ÷ MPS.
  • Fiscal policy: government spending and taxes. Expansionary (more spending, lower taxes) for recessions; contractionary for inflation.
Multiplier

MPC = 0.8, so MPS = 0.2 and the spending multiplier is 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5. A 10 billion dollar increase in government spending can raise GDP by up to 50 billion dollars.

Stagflation (high inflation and unemployment together) comes from a leftward shift of SRAS, such as an oil price spike.
