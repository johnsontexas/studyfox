Unit 4: The Financial Sector

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Macroeconomics · Lesson 5 of 7 · 9 min read

  • Money is a medium of exchange, unit of account, and store of value. M1 = currency + checkable deposits (and savings deposits since 2020); M2 = M1 + small time deposits and money market funds.
  • Fractional reserve banking: banks keep a fraction as reserves and lend the rest. Money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve requirement.
  • Monetary policy (the Fed): tools include open market operations (buying or selling bonds), the discount rate, interest on reserves (the main tool today, with ample reserves), and reserve requirements.
  • The money market: money demand slopes down, and money supply is vertical (set by the Fed). Increasing the money supply lowers the nominal interest rate.
  • Bond prices and interest rates move in opposite directions.
  • Loanable funds market: determines the real interest rate. Government borrowing increases demand for loanable funds, raising rates and crowding out private investment.
Expansionary monetary chain: Fed buys bonds, money supply up, interest rates down, investment up, AD right, output and price level up.
