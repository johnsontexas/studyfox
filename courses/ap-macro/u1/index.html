Unit 1: Basic Economic Concepts

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Macroeconomics · Lesson 2 of 7 · 7 min read

  • Scarcity: unlimited wants, limited resources. Every choice has an opportunity cost, the value of the next-best alternative.
  • Production possibilities curve (PPC): points on the curve are efficient, inside are inefficient, outside are unattainable. Economic growth shifts it outward.
  • Comparative advantage: produce what you have the lower opportunity cost for, then trade. Both sides gain.
  • Supply and demand: demand slopes down, supply slopes up. Shifters of demand include income, tastes, prices of related goods, expectations, and number of buyers.
Comparative advantage

In one hour, Ana makes 4 pies or 8 cakes; Ben makes 2 pies or 6 cakes. Ana's cost of 1 pie is 2 cakes; Ben's is 3 cakes. Ana has the comparative advantage in pies, and Ben in cakes (his cost of a cake is 1/3 pie vs. Ana's 1/2 pie).

Check your understanding

What does a point inside the PPC represent?

  1. Economic growth
  2. Unemployed or inefficiently used resources
  3. An unattainable level of output
  4. Maximum efficiency
Show answer

Answer: B. The economy could produce more of both goods, so resources are idle or wasted.
