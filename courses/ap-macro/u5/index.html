Unit 5: Long-Run Consequences of Stabilization Policies

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Macroeconomics · Lesson 6 of 7 · 7 min read

  • The Phillips curve: in the short run, lower unemployment comes with higher inflation (a downward-sloping SRPC). In the long run, the LRPC is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment.
  • An increase in AD moves the economy up along the SRPC; a supply shock shifts the SRPC.
  • Money growth and inflation: the quantity theory, MV = PQ. In the long run, faster money growth leads to inflation.
  • Deficits and debt: a deficit is one year's shortfall; the debt is the total accumulated. Large deficits can crowd out investment and slow long-run growth.
  • Economic growth comes from more and better capital (physical and human), technology, and productivity. Shown as LRAS and the PPC shifting right.

Answer: C. In the long run, there is no tradeoff: unemployment returns to its natural rate whatever the inflation rate.
