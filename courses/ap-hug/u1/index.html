Unit 1: Thinking Geographically

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Human Geography · Lesson 1 of 7 · 7 min read

The exam: 60 multiple-choice questions (60 min, 50%) and 3 free-response questions (75 min, 50%), two of which use a map, chart, or image.

  • Maps: all map projections distort shape, area, distance, or direction (the Mercator enlarges areas near the poles). Map types include choropleth, dot density, graduated symbol, isoline, and cartogram.
  • Location: absolute (coordinates) vs. relative (in relation to other places). Site is the physical character of a place; situation is its location relative to other places.
  • Scale: patterns look different at global, national, regional, and local scales.
  • Regions: formal (shared trait), functional (organized around a node, like a city's commuter area), and perceptual or vernacular (people's mental image, like "the South").
  • Spatial concepts: distance decay, time-space compression, and diffusion (relocation, hierarchical, contagious, stimulus).
  • Data: GIS, GPS, remote sensing, and field observation.

Check your understanding

A trend that spreads from large cities to smaller cities, skipping rural areas, shows which type of diffusion?

  1. Contagious
  2. Hierarchical
  3. Relocation
  4. Stimulus
Show answer

Answer: B. Hierarchical diffusion moves from more important or connected places down to less important ones.
