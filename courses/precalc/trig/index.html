Trigonometry and the unit circle

Algebra 2 & Precalculus · Lesson 4 of 4 · 8 min read

Right-triangle trig

SOH-CAH-TOA: $\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}$, $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}$.

Radians

$180^\circ = \pi$ radians. Multiply degrees by $\frac{\pi}{180}$ to convert.

The unit circle

A point on the unit circle at angle $\theta$ is $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.
$\theta$$0$$\frac{\pi}{6}$$\frac{\pi}{4}$$\frac{\pi}{3}$$\frac{\pi}{2}$
$\sin$$0$$\frac{1}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$1$
$\cos$$1$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$\frac{1}{2}$$0$

Use signs by quadrant for other angles. Key identity: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.

The graph of $y = a\sin(bx)$ has amplitude $|a|$ and period $\frac{2\pi}{b}$.

What is $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)$?

  1. $\frac{1}{2}$
  2. $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
  3. $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
  4. $1$
Show answer

Answer: A. $\frac{\pi}{6} = 30^\circ$, and $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$.
