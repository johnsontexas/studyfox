Writing Strong Essays · Lesson 4 of 6 · 5 min read

Introduction

  1. Hook: a question, surprising fact, or brief scene. Skip "Since the dawn of time."
  2. Context: the title, author, or background the reader needs.
  3. Thesis: usually the last sentence.

Conclusion

Don't just repeat your thesis. Answer "So what?": why does your argument matter, or what bigger idea does it connect to?
Tip Many writers draft the introduction last, once they know exactly what the essay argues.

Check your understanding

What should a strong conclusion do?

  1. Copy the introduction word for word
  2. Introduce brand-new evidence
  3. Show why the argument matters beyond the essay
  4. Apologize for any weak points
Show answer

Answer: C. A conclusion wraps up by showing the significance of your argument, not by repeating or adding new evidence.
