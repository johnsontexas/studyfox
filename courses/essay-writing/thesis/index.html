Writing a thesis statement

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Writing Strong Essays · Lesson 1 of 6 · 6 min read

Your thesis is the one-sentence answer to the essay question. Every paragraph exists to prove it.

A strong thesis is arguable (someone could disagree), specific, and hints at your reasons.
IncorrectSocial media is used by many teenagers.

That's a fact, not an argument. Nobody would disagree.

IncorrectSocial media is bad.

Arguable, but vague. Bad how, and for whom?

CorrectSchools should limit phone use during class because constant notifications weaken focus and make it harder for students to retain what they learn.
Tip Write your thesis as an answer to "What do I think, and why?" If you can't finish the "because," you need more thinking before you write.

Check your understanding

Which is the strongest thesis?

  1. The Great Gatsby was written by F. Scott Fitzgerald in 1925.
  2. The Great Gatsby is an interesting book with many themes.
  3. In The Great Gatsby, Fitzgerald uses Gatsby's parties to show that wealth can buy attention but not belonging.
  4. This essay will discuss The Great Gatsby.
Show answer

Answer: C. It makes a specific, arguable claim and points to the evidence (the parties). The others are facts, vague, or announcements.
