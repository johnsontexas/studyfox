Body paragraphs: claim, evidence, analysis

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Writing Strong Essays · Lesson 2 of 6 · 7 min read

A strong body paragraph has three parts:

  1. Claim: a topic sentence that states one reason supporting your thesis.
  2. Evidence: a quote, fact, or example that backs up the claim.
  3. Analysis: your explanation of how the evidence proves the claim. This is where the grade is.
Aim for at least as much analysis as evidence. Evidence shows what; analysis explains why it matters.
Example

Claim: Gatsby's parties show that his wealth attracts crowds but not real connection. Evidence: Nick says he was "one of the few guests who had actually been invited." Analysis: Guests treat the mansion like a public place, not a friend's home, so the parties that are meant to prove Gatsby belongs actually show how alone he is.

Tip End each paragraph by connecting back to the thesis, not by starting the next topic.

Check your understanding

In a body paragraph, what does analysis do?

  1. Summarizes the plot
  2. Explains how the evidence supports the claim
  3. Introduces a new topic
  4. Repeats the quote in different words
Show answer

Answer: B. Analysis connects evidence to your claim. Summarizing or paraphrasing the quote isn't analysis.
