Writing Strong Essays · Lesson 5 of 6 · 6 min read

  • Topic vs. theme: "love" is a topic. A theme is a full statement: "Love can blind people to the truth."
  • Characterization: what characters say, do, and think, and how others react to them.
  • Literary devices: symbolism, imagery, metaphor, irony, foreshadowing, and point of view.
Don't just name a device. Explain what effect it has and how it builds the theme.
IncorrectThe author uses a lot of imagery.
CorrectThe repeated image of the green light turns Gatsby's dream into something visible but always out of reach.
Tip Take notes while reading: jot down repeated images, big choices characters make, and passages that surprise you. Those become your evidence.

Check your understanding

Which is a theme, rather than a topic?

  1. Friendship
  2. Ambition
  3. Unchecked ambition can destroy the people closest to us.
  4. The 1920s
Show answer

Answer: C. A theme is a complete idea about life. Single words like "friendship" or "ambition" are topics.
