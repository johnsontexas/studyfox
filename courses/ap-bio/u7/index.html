Unit 7: Natural Selection

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Biology · Lesson 7 of 8 · 8 min read

  • Natural selection: variation exists, it is heritable, more offspring are produced than survive, and individuals with favorable traits reproduce more. Populations evolve, not individuals.
  • Other mechanisms: genetic drift (random, strongest in small populations: the bottleneck and founder effects), gene flow, mutation, and nonrandom mating.
  • Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium: $p + q = 1$ and $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$. It holds only with no selection, no mutation, no migration, random mating, and a large population.
  • Evidence for evolution: fossils, homologous structures, DNA and protein comparisons, and biogeography.
  • Phylogenetic trees show common ancestry. Branch points are common ancestors.
  • Speciation: allopatric (geographic separation) and sympatric. Prezygotic barriers (habitat, timing, behavior) and postzygotic barriers (hybrid sterility).
Hardy-Weinberg

16% of a population shows a recessive trait, so $q^2 = 0.16$ and $q = 0.4$. Then $p = 0.6$, and heterozygous carriers are $2pq = 0.48$, or 48%.

Check your understanding

If 9% of a population is homozygous recessive, what fraction is heterozygous?

  1. 0.09
  2. 0.21
  3. 0.42
  4. 0.49
Show answer

Answer: C. $q^2 = 0.09$, so $q = 0.3$ and $p = 0.7$. Then $2pq = 2(0.7)(0.3) = 0.42$.
