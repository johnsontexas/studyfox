Unit 3: Cellular Energetics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Biology · Lesson 3 of 8 · 9 min read

  • Enzymes lower activation energy. The substrate fits the active site. Temperature and pH changes can denature enzymes. Competitive inhibitors block the active site; noncompetitive inhibitors bind elsewhere and change the shape.
  • Photosynthesis (in chloroplasts):
    • Light reactions (thylakoid membrane): light splits water, releasing oxygen. The electron transport chain pumps H⁺ into the thylakoid; ATP synthase makes ATP. NADPH is made.
    • Calvin cycle (stroma): uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into sugar.
  • Cellular respiration:
    • Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose to 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP.
    • Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix): makes NADH, FADH₂, and CO₂.
    • Oxidative phosphorylation (inner membrane): the electron transport chain pumps H⁺; ATP synthase uses the gradient to make most of the ATP. Oxygen is the final electron acceptor, forming water.
  • Without oxygen, fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue.
Chemiosmosis is the same idea in both organelles: an electron transport chain builds an H⁺ gradient, and ATP synthase uses it to make ATP.
