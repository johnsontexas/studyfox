Unit 1: Chemistry of Life

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Biology · Lesson 1 of 8 · 8 min read

The exam: 60 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 50%) and 6 free-response questions (90 min, 50%): 2 long and 4 short. A four-function or scientific calculator and a formula sheet are allowed.

  • Water: polar, with hydrogen bonds that give it cohesion, adhesion, high specific heat, and the ability to dissolve polar substances.
  • Macromolecules are built by dehydration synthesis and broken by hydrolysis:
    • Carbohydrates (C, H, O): monosaccharides, glycosidic linkages. Energy and structure (cellulose).
    • Lipids: nonpolar. Phospholipids have hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails. Saturated (no double bonds) vs. unsaturated.
    • Proteins (C, H, O, N, S): amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Primary, secondary (hydrogen bonds in the backbone), tertiary (R-group interactions), and quaternary structure. Shape determines function.
    • Nucleic acids (C, H, O, N, P): nucleotides. DNA has deoxyribose and thymine; RNA has ribose and uracil. Strands are antiparallel (5' to 3').
Structure determines function. If a question changes a molecule's shape (like a mutation that changes an R group), predict how its function changes.

Check your understanding

Which level of protein structure is determined by interactions between R groups?

  1. Primary
  2. Secondary
  3. Tertiary
  4. None; R groups don't interact
Show answer

Answer: C. Tertiary structure comes from R-group interactions: hydrophobic interactions, ionic bonds, hydrogen bonds, and disulfide bridges.
