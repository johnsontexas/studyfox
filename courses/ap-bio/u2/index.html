Unit 2: Cell Structure and Function

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Biology · Lesson 2 of 8 · 8 min read

  • Organelles: ribosomes (protein synthesis), rough ER (proteins for export), smooth ER (lipids, detox), Golgi (modifies and ships proteins), lysosomes (digestion), mitochondria (cellular respiration), chloroplasts (photosynthesis), and the vacuole.
  • Endosymbiotic theory: mitochondria and chloroplasts were once free-living prokaryotes. Evidence: double membranes, their own circular DNA, and their own ribosomes.
  • Surface area to volume ratio: as a cell grows, volume increases faster than surface area, limiting exchange. That's why cells are small.
  • The membrane: the fluid mosaic model, selectively permeable. Small nonpolar molecules diffuse through; ions and large polar molecules need proteins.
  • Transport: passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) needs no energy. Active transport (like the sodium-potassium pump) uses ATP to move against a gradient. Endocytosis and exocytosis move bulk material.
  • Tonicity: in a hypotonic solution water enters the cell; in a hypertonic one water leaves. Water potential: water moves from higher to lower water potential.
