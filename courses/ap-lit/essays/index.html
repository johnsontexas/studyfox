Writing the three literature essays

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP English Literature and Composition · Lesson 6 of 6 · 8 min read

Poetry and prose analysis (Q1, Q2)

  • The prompt names the focus (for example, "how the author portrays the complex relationship between a father and son"). Your thesis must answer it with an interpretation.
  • Organize by ideas or by sections of the passage, not by device ("paragraph on imagery, paragraph on diction").
  • Embed short quotes and explain how the language creates the meaning.

Literary argument (Q3)

  • Pick a work that clearly fits the prompt. Use specific scenes and moments, not plot summary.
  • Connect every paragraph to how the idea in the prompt shapes the meaning of the work as a whole.
Plot summary earns no points. Every paragraph should argue an interpretation and prove it with analysis.

Check your understanding

Why should you avoid organizing a literary analysis by device (one paragraph on imagery, one on diction)?

  1. Devices aren't allowed on the AP exam
  2. It tends to produce a list instead of an argument about meaning
  3. It makes the essay too short
  4. Graders only read the first paragraph
Show answer

Answer: B. Organizing by ideas keeps the essay focused on interpretation, with devices as evidence.
