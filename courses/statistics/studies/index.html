Sampling, experiments, and causation

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Statistics & Probability · Lesson 4 of 4 · 5 min read

  • Correlation is not causation. Two variables can move together because of a third, hidden variable.
  • An observational study only watches. An experiment assigns treatments.
  • Random assignment in an experiment is what allows cause-and-effect conclusions.
  • Random sampling is what allows you to generalize to the whole population.

Common biases

Voluntary response (only people who care respond), convenience samples, and leading questions all make results unreliable, no matter how large the sample.

Which study design can show that a new study method causes higher grades?

  1. A survey of students who chose the method
  2. A randomized experiment that assigns students to methods
  3. Comparing grades at two different schools
  4. A large online poll

Answer: B. Only random assignment balances out other differences between groups, so any difference can be attributed to the method.
