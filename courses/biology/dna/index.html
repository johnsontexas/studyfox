DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Biology Essentials · Lesson 3 of 5 · 7 min read

DNA stores the instructions for building proteins. Its bases pair up: A with T and C with G.

From DNA to protein

  1. Transcription (in the nucleus): DNA is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA). RNA uses U instead of T, so A pairs with U.
  2. Translation (at a ribosome): the mRNA is read three bases at a time. Each three-base codon codes for an amino acid, and the chain of amino acids folds into a protein.
DNA → RNA → protein. This is called the central dogma of biology.

A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence. It can change the protein, sometimes harmlessly and sometimes not.

A DNA strand reads A-T-G-C. What is the complementary DNA strand?

  1. T-A-C-G
  2. U-A-C-G
  3. A-T-G-C
  4. G-C-A-T
Show answer

Answer: A. A pairs with T and C pairs with G, so ATGC pairs with TACG. (U appears only in RNA.)
