Motion and kinematics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Physics Essentials · Lesson 1 of 5 · 7 min read

  • Velocity is speed with a direction. Acceleration is how fast velocity changes.
  • Near Earth, falling objects accelerate at $g \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ (ignoring air resistance).

Kinematics equations (constant acceleration)

  • $v = v_0 + at$
  • $\Delta x = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
  • $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x$
Problem steps: list what you know ($v_0$, $v$, $a$, $t$, $\Delta x$), circle what you need, and pick the equation that uses only those.
Example

A car starts from rest and accelerates at $3\text{ m/s}^2$ for 4 s. Speed: $v = 0 + 3(4) = 12$ m/s. Distance: $\frac{1}{2}(3)(4^2) = 24$ m.

Check your understanding

A ball is dropped from rest. Ignoring air resistance, how fast is it moving after 2 seconds?

  1. $4.9$ m/s
  2. $9.8$ m/s
  3. $19.6$ m/s
  4. $39.2$ m/s
Show answer

Answer: C. $v = v_0 + at = 0 + 9.8(2) = 19.6$ m/s.
