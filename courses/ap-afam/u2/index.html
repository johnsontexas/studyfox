Unit 2: Freedom, Enslavement, and Resistance

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP African American Studies · Lesson 3 of 5 · 8 min read

  • The Middle Passage: the brutal voyage across the Atlantic. About 12.5 million Africans were forced onto ships; about 10.7 million survived.
  • Slavery in the U.S.: slavery became hereditary and race-based through colonial law. The domestic slave trade split families as cotton expanded.
  • Culture as resistance: spirituals, family bonds, religion, and storytelling preserved identity.
  • Resistance: everyday resistance (slowing work), escape (the Underground Railroad, Harriet Tubman), and revolts (the Stono Rebellion 1739, Nat Turner 1831).
  • Abolitionists: Frederick Douglass, Sojourner Truth, David Walker, and many free Black communities in the North.
  • The Civil War and emancipation: enslaved people freed themselves by fleeing to Union lines; Black soldiers fought for the Union; the 13th Amendment (1865).
