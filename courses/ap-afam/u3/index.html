Unit 3: The Practice of Freedom

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP African American Studies · Lesson 4 of 5 · 8 min read

  • Reconstruction: Black Americans built schools, churches, and businesses, and held office. The 14th and 15th Amendments promised citizenship and the vote.
  • Backlash: Black Codes, the Ku Klux Klan, sharecropping, and Jim Crow segregation, upheld by Plessy v. Ferguson (1896). Lynching terrorized communities; Ida B. Wells campaigned against it.
  • Debates over strategy: Booker T. Washington urged economic self-help and vocational education; W.E.B. Du Bois demanded full civil rights and higher education (and helped found the NAACP in 1909).
  • The Great Migration: millions moved from the rural South to Northern and Western cities.
  • The Harlem Renaissance (1920s): writers and artists like Langston Hughes, Zora Neale Hurston, and Aaron Douglas celebrated Black culture. Jazz and blues spread nationwide.
  • Marcus Garvey promoted Black pride and Pan-Africanism.
