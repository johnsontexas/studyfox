Learning reading

SAT & ACT Study Strategy · Lesson 3 of 6 · 6 min read

Reading is a skill you build with a process, not facts you memorize.

A process for every reading question

  1. Read the question first so you know what to look for.
  2. Read the passage for its main point. Summarize it in a few words in your head.
  3. Predict the answer before looking at the choices.
  4. Find evidence. The right answer is always supported by the text, not by outside knowledge.
  5. Eliminate choices that are too extreme, too broad, too narrow, or only partly true.

Vocabulary

For Words in Context, cover the blank and put in your own simple word, then find the closest match. Build vocabulary by making flashcards from words you miss, with the sentence you saw them in.

Practice outside the test

Reading a few articles a week (science news, history, opinion pieces) builds the speed and stamina that practice questions alone can't.

Check your understanding

On a reading question, when should you predict the answer?

  1. After reading all four choices
  2. Before looking at the choices
  3. Only on hard questions
  4. Never; just go with what sounds right
Show answer

Answer: B. Predicting first keeps attractive but unsupported choices from pulling you in.
