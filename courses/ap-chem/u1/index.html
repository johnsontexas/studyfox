Unit 1: Atomic Structure and Properties

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Chemistry · Lesson 1 of 9 · 8 min read

The exam: 60 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 50%) and 7 free-response questions (105 min, 50%): 3 long and 4 short. A calculator, periodic table, and formula sheet are allowed.

  • The mole: $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles. Moles = mass ÷ molar mass.
  • Mass spectra show isotopes and their relative abundance; the average atomic mass is a weighted average.
  • Electron configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d¹⁰... Photoelectron spectroscopy (PES) shows energy needed to remove electrons from each subshell; peak height is the number of electrons.
  • Coulomb's law explains periodic trends: the force increases with charge and decreases with distance.
  • Trends: atomic radius decreases across a period (more protons, same shell) and increases down a group (more shells). Ionization energy and electronegativity do the opposite.

Check your understanding

Why does atomic radius decrease from left to right across a period?

  1. Electrons are added to new shells
  2. Increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons in the same shell closer
  3. Atoms lose neutrons
  4. Shielding increases greatly
Show answer

Answer: B. Protons are added while electrons go into the same shell, so the stronger effective nuclear charge pulls them closer.
