Unit 3: Intermolecular Forces and Properties

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Chemistry · Lesson 3 of 9 · 9 min read

  • Intermolecular forces (from weakest to strongest, generally): London dispersion (all molecules, stronger with more electrons), dipole-dipole (polar molecules), and hydrogen bonding (H bonded to N, O, or F).
  • Stronger IMFs mean higher boiling points and lower vapor pressure.
  • Ideal gas law: $PV = nRT$. Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • Kinetic molecular theory: average kinetic energy is proportional to temperature (Kelvin).
  • Solutions: "like dissolves like." Molarity $M = \frac{\text{mol solute}}{\text{L solution}}$. Separation methods include chromatography and distillation.
  • Beer-Lambert law: $A = \varepsilon b c$; absorbance is proportional to concentration.
Ideal gas law

How many moles are in 2.0 L of gas at 1.0 atm and 300 K? $n = \frac{PV}{RT} = \frac{(1.0)(2.0)}{(0.0821)(300)} \approx 0.081$ mol.
