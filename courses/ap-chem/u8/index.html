Unit 8: Acids and Bases

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Chemistry · Lesson 8 of 9 · 9 min read

  • $\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$, and $\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$ at 25°C.
  • Strong acids (HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄) ionize completely. Weak acids partially ionize: use Ka and an ICE table.
  • Buffers are a weak acid with its conjugate base. They resist pH change. Henderson-Hasselbalch: $\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}$.
  • At the half-equivalence point of a weak acid titration, pH = pKa.
  • Titration curves: a strong acid with a strong base has an equivalence point at pH 7; a weak acid with a strong base has an equivalence point above 7.

0.010 M HCl: $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] = 0.010$, so pH = 2.

During the titration of a weak acid, at the half-equivalence point:

  2. pH = pKa

  4. All the acid is gone

Answer: B. Half the acid has been converted to its conjugate base, so [A⁻] = [HA] and the log term is zero.
