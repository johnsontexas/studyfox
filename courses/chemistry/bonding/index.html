Chemical bonding and formulas

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

Chemistry Essentials · Lesson 2 of 5 · 6 min read

  • Ionic bonds: a metal gives electrons to a nonmetal, forming oppositely charged ions (like NaCl).
  • Covalent bonds: nonmetals share electrons (like H2O).
  • Metallic bonds: metal atoms share a "sea" of electrons, which is why metals conduct electricity.

Writing ionic formulas

The total charge must be zero. Use the criss-cross method: each ion's charge becomes the other ion's subscript, then reduce.
Examples

Mg2+ and Cl− → MgCl2. Al3+ and O2− → Al2O3.

What is the formula for the compound of Ca2+ and Cl−?

  1. CaCl
  2. CaCl2
  3. Ca2Cl
  4. Ca2Cl2
Show answer

Answer: B. Two Cl− ions (total −2) balance one Ca2+, giving CaCl2.
