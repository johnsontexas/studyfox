Kinematics with calculus

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Mechanics · Lesson 1 of 6 · 8 min read

The exam: 40 multiple-choice questions (80 min, 50%) and 4 free-response questions (100 min, 50%). You should be taking or have taken calculus.

  • $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$ and $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$.
  • Going backward: $x(t) = x_0 + \int_0^t v\,dt$ and $v(t) = v_0 + \int_0^t a\,dt$.
  • The constant-acceleration equations only work when $a$ is constant. If $a$ depends on time, integrate.
  • If $a$ depends on velocity, separate variables: $\frac{dv}{dt} = -bv$ gives $v = v_0e^{-bt}$.
Example

$x(t) = 2t^3 - 4t$. Then $v = 6t^2 - 4$ and $a = 12t$. At $t = 1$ s: $v = 2$ m/s and $a = 12$ m/s².

Check your understanding

If $x(t) = 3t^2 + 2t$, what is the acceleration?

  1. $6t + 2$
  2. $6$
  3. $3$
  4. $2$
Show answer

Answer: B. $v = 6t + 2$ and $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6$, a constant.
