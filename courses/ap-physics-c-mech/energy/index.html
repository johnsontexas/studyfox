Work, energy, and power with variable forces

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Mechanics · Lesson 3 of 6 · 8 min read

  • Work by a variable force: $W = \int \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$, the area under F vs. x.
  • Potential energy and force: $F_x = -\frac{dU}{dx}$. Equilibrium points are where $\frac{dU}{dx} = 0$: stable at minimums of U, unstable at maximums.
  • Gravitational potential energy (general): $U = -\frac{GMm}{r}$. Escape speed: $v = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$.
  • Power: $P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$.
Example

A spring with $F = -kx$: $W = \int_0^x kx\,dx = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$, the work to stretch it, stored as potential energy.

Check your understanding

If $U(x) = x^2 - 4x$, where is the equilibrium point?

  1. $x = 0$
  2. $x = 2$
  3. $x = 4$
  4. $x = -2$
Show answer

Answer: B. $\frac{dU}{dx} = 2x - 4 = 0$ gives $x = 2$. It's a minimum of U, so it's stable.
