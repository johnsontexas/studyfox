Linear momentum and impulse

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Mechanics · Lesson 4 of 6 · 7 min read

  • Impulse: $J = \int F\,dt = \Delta p$.
  • Momentum is conserved when no net external force acts on the system.
  • Center of mass velocity: $v_{cm} = \frac{\Sigma m_iv_i}{\Sigma m_i}$. External forces alone change the center of mass motion.
  • Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy; in one dimension, relative speed of approach equals relative speed of separation.
Example

$F(t) = 6t$ N acts from $t = 0$ to $t = 2$ s on a 3 kg object at rest. $J = \int_0^2 6t\,dt = 12$ N·s, so $v = 4$ m/s.

Check your understanding

A force $F(t) = 4t$ acts from $t = 0$ to $t = 3$ s. What is the impulse?

  1. 12 N·s
  2. 18 N·s
  3. 36 N·s
  4. 4 N·s
Show answer

Answer: B. $\int_0^3 4t\,dt = 2t^2\big|_0^3 = 18$ N·s.
