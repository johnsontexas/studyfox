Forces, drag, and translational dynamics

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Physics C: Mechanics · Lesson 2 of 6 · 9 min read

  • $\Sigma F = ma = m\frac{dv}{dt}$. More generally, $\Sigma F = \frac{dp}{dt}$.
  • Drag (resistive) forces like $F = -bv$ lead to differential equations. A falling object approaches terminal velocity when drag equals weight: $v_T = \frac{mg}{b}$.
  • For $mg - bv = m\frac{dv}{dt}$: $v(t) = \frac{mg}{b}(1 - e^{-bt/m})$.
  • Circular motion and gravitation as in Physics 1, plus orbital speed $v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$.
  • Center of mass of a continuous object: $x_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\int x\,dm$.

Check your understanding

For an object falling with drag force $bv$, what is its terminal velocity?

  1. $mg$
  2. $\frac{mg}{b}$
  3. $\frac{b}{mg}$
  4. $\sqrt{mg}$
Show answer

Answer: B. At terminal velocity, acceleration is zero, so $mg = bv_T$ and $v_T = mg/b$.
