Semicolons and colons

SAT Grammar Rules · Lesson 2 of 9 · 5 min read

Semicolons

A semicolon works exactly like a period. Both sides must be independent clauses.
IncorrectThe experiment failed; because the sample was contaminated.
CorrectThe experiment failed; the sample was contaminated.

(A semicolon can also separate items in a list when the items already contain commas, but that's rare on the SAT.)

Colons

The part before a colon must be a complete sentence. What follows can be a list, an explanation, or a single word.
IncorrectThe recipe requires: flour, eggs, and butter.
CorrectThe recipe requires three ingredients: flour, eggs, and butter.
CorrectHer conclusion was simple: the bridge needed repairs.

Never put a colon right after a verb or after "such as," "including," or "like."

Check your understanding

Choose the correct option: "The expedition carried only the essentials ______ water, maps, and a radio."

  1. essentials:
  2. essentials;
  3. essentials, such as:
  4. essentials
Show answer

Answer: A. The first part is a complete sentence and what follows is a list that explains it, which is exactly the colon's job. A semicolon needs an independent clause after it.
