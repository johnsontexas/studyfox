Commas: extra information and lists

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Grammar Rules · Lesson 3 of 9 · 7 min read

Nonessential information

Extra information that could be removed without breaking the sentence goes between a matching pair of commas, dashes, or parentheses.
IncorrectMarie Curie, who won two Nobel Prizes was born in Warsaw.
CorrectMarie Curie, who won two Nobel Prizes, was born in Warsaw.

Test: remove the part between the commas. If the sentence still works, the commas are right.

Essential information gets no commas

CorrectThe novelist Toni Morrison won the Nobel Prize in 1993.

Without "Toni Morrison," we wouldn't know which novelist, so the name is essential and takes no commas. Titles and job descriptions right before a name ("biologist Rachel Carson") work the same way.

Never put a single comma...

  • between a subject and its verb (not "The results of the study, surprised everyone")
  • between a verb and its object
  • before that when it starts a clause
  • between an adjective and the noun it describes

Lists and introductions

Use commas between three or more items, and after an introductory phrase: "After the storm passed, the crew returned."

Check your understanding

Choose the correct option: "The engineer ______ designed the bridge will present tomorrow."

  1. engineer, who
  2. engineer who
  3. engineer who,
  4. engineer, who,
Show answer

Answer: B. "Who designed the bridge" tells us which engineer, so it is essential and takes no commas. Also, a comma cannot separate the subject "engineer" from its verb "will present."
