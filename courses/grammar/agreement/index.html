Subject-verb agreement

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Grammar Rules · Lesson 5 of 9 · 5 min read

A verb must match its subject, not the noun closest to it.

The SAT hides the subject behind a long phrase:

IncorrectThe collection of rare manuscripts are stored in a vault.
CorrectThe collection of rare manuscripts is stored in a vault.

Cross out prepositional phrases (of rare manuscripts) and the subject is clear: collection ... is.

Watch for

  • Collective nouns (team, committee, group, species) are usually singular.
  • Each, every, either, neither, everyone are singular.
  • Inverted sentences: "Among the findings were two new species." The subject (species) comes after the verb.
  • Compound subjects joined by and are plural.

Check your understanding

Choose the correct option: "The flock of migrating geese ______ south every autumn."

  1. fly
  2. flies
  3. have flown
  4. are flying
Show answer

Answer: B. The subject is "flock," which is singular. "Of migrating geese" is a prepositional phrase and doesn't change the subject.
