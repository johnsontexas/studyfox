Punctuating "however" and other transitions

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

SAT Grammar Rules · Lesson 4 of 9 · 4 min read

Words like however, therefore, moreover, consequently, and for example are not FANBOYS conjunctions. They can't join two sentences with just a comma.

Between two independent clauses: semicolon (or period) before, comma after. Inside a single clause: commas on both sides.
IncorrectThe data were promising, however, more trials are needed.
CorrectThe data were promising; however, more trials are needed.
CorrectThe data were promising. More trials, however, are needed.
Tip When the choices differ only in where the punctuation goes around a transition word, first find the two clauses. The transition belongs to the second one.

Check your understanding

Choose the correct option: "Sales rose in March ______ they fell sharply in April."

  1. March, however,
  2. March; however,
  3. March however;
  4. March, however
Show answer

Answer: B. Both sides are independent clauses, so the transition needs a semicolon before it and a comma after it.
