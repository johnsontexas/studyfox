SAT Grammar Rules · Lesson 8 of 9 · 4 min read

An introductory phrase describes whatever comes right after the comma.
IncorrectHaving studied for weeks, the exam felt easy.

That says the exam studied for weeks.

CorrectHaving studied for weeks, Maya found the exam easy.

On the SAT, these questions usually give you the introductory phrase and ask you to pick how the sentence continues. Pick the choice that puts the right noun first.

Tip Ask "who or what is doing the action in the opening phrase?" The answer must be the first noun after the comma.

Check your understanding

"Built in the twelfth century, ______" Which choice completes the sentence correctly?

  1. visitors still admire the cathedral's stained glass.
  2. the cathedral still draws thousands of visitors.
  3. thousands of visitors still come to see the cathedral.
  4. it is still admired, the cathedral.
Show answer

Answer: B. The cathedral was built in the twelfth century, so "the cathedral" must come right after the comma.
