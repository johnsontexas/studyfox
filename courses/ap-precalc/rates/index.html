Unit 1A: Functions and rates of change

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Precalculus · Lesson 2 of 9 · 7 min read

  • Increasing and decreasing: a function is increasing on an interval if output rises as input rises.
  • Average rate of change on $[a, b]$ is $\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$, the slope of the secant line.
  • Concavity: concave up when the rate of change is increasing (the graph bends upward); concave down when it is decreasing.
  • Points of inflection are where concavity changes.
  • Linear functions have a constant rate of change; quadratic functions have rates of change that change at a constant rate.
Example

$f(x) = x^2$ on $[1, 3]$: average rate of change $= \frac{9 - 1}{3 - 1} = 4$.

Check your understanding

What is the average rate of change of $f(x) = x^3$ on $[0, 2]$?

  1. $2$
  2. $4$
  3. $6$
  4. $8$
Show answer

Answer: B. $\frac{f(2) - f(0)}{2 - 0} = \frac{8 - 0}{2} = 4$.
