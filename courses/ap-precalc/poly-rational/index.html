Unit 1B: Polynomial and rational functions

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

AP Precalculus · Lesson 3 of 9 · 9 min read

Polynomials

  • A degree-n polynomial has at most n real zeros and at most n − 1 turning points.
  • Multiplicity: an odd-multiplicity zero crosses the x-axis; an even one touches and turns.
  • End behavior depends on the leading term: even degree with positive leading coefficient goes up on both ends.
  • Complex zeros come in conjugate pairs.

Rational functions

  • Vertical asymptotes at zeros of the denominator that don't cancel. Zeros that cancel make holes.
  • Horizontal asymptotes: compare degrees. Bottom bigger: $y = 0$. Same: ratio of leading coefficients. Top bigger by one: a slant asymptote.
Example

$r(x) = \frac{(x - 2)(x + 1)}{(x - 2)(x - 3)}$ has a hole at $x = 2$, a vertical asymptote at $x = 3$, and a horizontal asymptote at $y = 1$.

Check your understanding

What is the horizontal asymptote of $\frac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 4}$?

  1. $y = 0$
  2. $y = 3$
  3. $y = \frac{1}{4}$
  4. None
Show answer

Answer: B. The degrees are equal, so the asymptote is the ratio of leading coefficients: $\frac{3}{1} = 3$.
