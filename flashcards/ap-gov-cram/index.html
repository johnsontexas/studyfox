Federalism
Power divided between national and state governments
Checks and balances
Each branch can limit the others
Federalist No. 10
Madison: a large republic controls the effects of factions
Federalist No. 51
Separation of powers and checks protect liberty
Brutus No. 1
Anti-Federalist warning that a strong central government threatens liberty
McCulloch v. Maryland
Necessary and proper clause allows a national bank; federal law is supreme
United States v. Lopez
Commerce clause does not cover guns near schools; limits federal power
Engel v. Vitale
School-sponsored prayer violates the establishment clause
Tinker v. Des Moines
Students keep free speech rights at school (armbands)
Gideon v. Wainwright
States must provide lawyers to poor defendants
Baker v. Carr
Courts can rule on redistricting; "one person, one vote"
Citizens United v. FEC
Independent political spending by corporations and unions is protected speech
Selective incorporation
Applying the Bill of Rights to states through the 14th Amendment
Filibuster
Senate tactic of extended debate to block a vote; ended by cloture (60 votes)
Pocket veto
Bill dies if the president doesn't sign it within 10 days after Congress adjourns
Judicial review
Power of courts to strike down unconstitutional laws
Linkage institutions
Parties, interest groups, elections, and media that connect people to government
Iron triangle
Relationship between a congressional committee, an agency, and an interest group
