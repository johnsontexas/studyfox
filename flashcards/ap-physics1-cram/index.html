Laws, relationships, and graph rules for AP Physics 1.

AP · Physics · 23 cards

Slope of x-t graph
Velocity
Slope of v-t graph
Acceleration
Area under v-t graph
Displacement
Free fall
Acceleration of g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward, ignoring air resistance
Projectile motion
Horizontal velocity is constant; vertical motion has acceleration g
Newton's first law
An object keeps its velocity unless a net force acts on it
Newton's second law
ΣF = ma
Newton's third law
Forces come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different objects
Normal force
Perpendicular push from a surface; not always equal to mg
Kinetic friction
f = μN, opposes sliding
Centripetal force
Net inward force mv²/r needed for circular motion
Work-energy theorem
Net work equals change in kinetic energy
Conservation of energy
With no friction or outside work, K + U stays constant
Power
Rate of doing work, P = W/t
Impulse
J = FΔt = Δp; the area under a force-time graph
Conservation of momentum
Total momentum is constant in any collision with no external force
Elastic collision
Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved
Perfectly inelastic collision
Objects stick together; momentum conserved, kinetic energy lost
Torque
τ = rF sin θ; causes angular acceleration
Rotational inertia
Resistance to angular acceleration; grows with mass far from the axis
Conservation of angular momentum
L = Iω stays constant with no external torque (spinning skater)
Simple harmonic motion
Restoring force proportional to displacement; spring period 2π√(m/k)
Pendulum period
2π√(L/g); independent of mass and small amplitude
