Grammar & Punctuation Rules

Open in StudyFox Free, no ads. Practice, track progress and quiz yourself in the app.

The Standard English Conventions rules tested on SAT Reading & Writing and ACT English.

SAT/ACT · Grammar · 20 cards

Semicolon (;)
Joins two complete sentences (independent clauses). Also separates list items that already contain commas.
Colon (:)
Must follow a complete sentence. Introduces a list, explanation, or example.
Comma splice
Error: two complete sentences joined by only a comma. Fix with a period, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS.
FANBOYS
For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So: use a comma BEFORE these to join two independent clauses.
Nonessential (extra) information
Set off with a matching pair: two commas, two dashes, or two parentheses. Never mix a comma and a dash.
Essential (restrictive) clause
Information needed to identify the noun takes NO commas. "The students who studied passed."
Subject-verb agreement
Find the true subject; ignore prepositional phrases and interrupters. "The box of apples IS heavy."
Each / every / either / neither
Singular: "Each of the students HAS a book."
Neither…nor / either…or
The verb agrees with the NEARER subject.
its vs. it's
its = possessive; it's = it is
their / there / they're
possessive / place / they are
Plural possessive
Add an apostrophe after the s: the birds' nests (many birds).
Pronoun agreement
A pronoun must match its antecedent in number: company → it; companies → they.
Dangling modifier
An opening phrase must describe the noun right after the comma. "Walking home, I saw…" not "Walking home, the rain…"
Past perfect (had + verb)
An action completed before another past action: "By 1917, they had built…"
Verb tense consistency
Keep tense consistent with the rest of the passage unless the timeline changes.
Parallel structure
Items in a list or comparison must share the same grammatical form.
Who vs. whom
who = subject (he); whom = object (him).
Apostrophe-free plurals
Plurals never take apostrophes: "the 1990s," "three cats."
Concision (ACT)
When all choices are correct, the shortest one that keeps the meaning is usually best.
