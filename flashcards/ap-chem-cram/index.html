Core ideas for every unit of AP Chem, in one fast review.

AP · Chemistry · 24 cards

Mole
6.022 × 10²³ particles; mass in grams ÷ molar mass
Limiting reactant
The reactant that runs out first and sets how much product forms
Coulomb's law
Attraction grows with charge and shrinks with distance; explains trends and lattice energy
Ionization energy trend
Increases up a group and across a period
Atomic radius trend
Increases down a group and decreases across a period
Electronegativity
Pull an atom has on shared electrons; highest at fluorine
VSEPR
Electron domains spread out to minimize repulsion, setting molecular shape
Intermolecular forces
London dispersion < dipole-dipole < hydrogen bonding; stronger IMFs mean higher boiling point
Ideal gas law
PV = nRT; real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature
Beer-Lambert law
A = εbc; absorbance is proportional to concentration
Net ionic equation
Shows only the species that actually react; spectator ions removed
Oxidation / reduction
OIL RIG: oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain
Rate law
rate = k[A]^m[B]^n; orders come from experiments, not coefficients
Half-life (first order)
t½ = 0.693/k, independent of concentration
Catalyst
Provides a lower-activation-energy path; does not change ΔH or K
Enthalpy (ΔH)
Heat of reaction at constant pressure; negative means exothermic
Gibbs free energy
ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; negative ΔG means thermodynamically favorable
Equilibrium constant K
Ratio of products to reactants at equilibrium; K > 1 favors products
Q vs. K
Q < K shifts right, Q > K shifts left, Q = K at equilibrium
Le Chatelier's principle
A system at equilibrium shifts to counteract a stress
Strong acids
HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃, H₂SO₄, HClO₄: ionize completely
Buffer
Weak acid plus its conjugate base; resists pH change. pH = pKa at half-equivalence
Ksp
Solubility product; common ions lower solubility
Galvanic cell
Spontaneous redox makes electricity; E°cell > 0, oxidation at the anode
